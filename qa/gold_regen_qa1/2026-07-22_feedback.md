## Per-card defects

**Trade 2 — Pivot (RANGE mean-reversion), SHORT, live**

8. Entry uses daily **R3** ($4,070.88) as the fade level, but the M5 rule for a RANGE Trade 2 short is
   "limit at R1/R1.5/R2 (short), tier by distance in ATR" — R3 is not a valid tier under this rule
   regardless of the pivot values used. This choice is a direct symptom of defect 1: because the printed
   (D-2) pivots are shifted well below current spot, R1 (4,031.03) and R2 (4,053.68) already sit *below*
   spot and can't serve as resistance, leaving only R3 above it. Once the daily pivots are corrected
   (defect 1), the compliant tier set is R1=$4,109.6367, R1.5(interpolated)≈$4,125.55, or R2=$4,141.4633 —
   all above spot and available for tiering by ATR distance.

9. TP3 ("Toward daily P $4,013.83") sits on the wrong side of TP1 ($4,010.29) for a SHORT: TP3 is *above*
   TP1, i.e. less favourable, and the static linter already flags this (`WARN_TP3_ORDER`). A compliant
   runner target must be beyond TP2 in the profitable (downward) direction, or left null. Once the daily
   pivots are corrected, "daily P" itself becomes $4,054.6933 — even further on the wrong side — so the
   runner should not target daily P at all; select a level beyond TP2 ($3,949.70) to the downside.

**Trade 3 — Mean-Reversion (3B, RANGE), LONG, live**

10. Stop loss is described as "low + 10% of range width ($50.00 / 5,000 ticks)" using the report's own
    stated range ($3,944 low, $438.62 width). 10% of $438.62 is $43.862, not $50.00 — the stop distance
    used does not match the formula cited for it (a ~$6.14 mislabel, i.e. the stop was effectively set to a
    round $50 rather than derived from 10% of the stated width). A compliant stop under the report's own
    numbers is $3,944 + $43.862 = **$3,987.86** (using the leak-free `swing_low_25d_full`=3,943.08 and
    width 439.14 instead: $3,943.08 + $43.914 = **$3,986.99**), not $3,988.09. Recompute the stop, and R
    and the tranche management that follows from it, once the correct 10%-of-width figure is used.
