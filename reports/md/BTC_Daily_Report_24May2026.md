The four Core observations (CoinDesk, CoinMarketCap, Yahoo/Coinbase, news.bitcoin.com/Bitstamp) cluster between roughly $74,600 and $75,900; the two Directional observations (CoinGecko at $77,008 and Twelve Data at $77,000) are earlier in the window and were down-weighted because Bitcoin fell materially after they were recorded.

The lower bound reflects the 23 May session low ($74,313) extended slightly for weekend slippage; the upper bound reflects the level Bitcoin would need to reclaim — the lost $76,000 floor approach — for the break to be in doubt. The final number is the most defensible because it is anchored to a completed, multi-source-corroborated candle close rather than to a single thin weekend tick.

# 12. Key Market Considerations
Structured fundamentals. Each factor is labelled price-supportive, price-negative or neutral, and tagged structural (6+ months) or cyclical (reversible within the horizon).
### Supply / on-chain — price-negative (cyclical)
Corporate-treasury buying — a major structural demand pillar through 2025 — fell roughly 80% month-on-month per Bitfinex commentary, narrowing net institutional demand almost entirely to the ETF channel. With that channel in outflow (see below), the marginal buyer has thinned. Post-halving issuance remains low (block reward 3.125 BTC) which is a structural positive, but it is being overwhelmed by the cyclical demand air-pocket. Net: price-negative.
### Demand / ETF flows — price-negative (cyclical, the dominant near-term factor)
US spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded five consecutive sessions of net outflows through 22 May. The 18 May session alone saw $648.6m leave — the third-largest single-day outflow of 2026 — with BlackRock's IBIT accounting for $448.4m of it; a further $105.2m exited on 22 May. The seven-day moving average of spot-ETF flows has turned negative for the first time since February. Crucially, these outflows occurred during a price recovery attempt, which analysts read as institutions using strength to exit rather than to accumulate. This is the single most important price-negative driver in the report.
### Substitution / spreads — neutral to mildly negative (cyclical)
Ethereum fell harder than Bitcoin over the window and Ether ETFs extended a six-session outflow streak, so there is no rotation into the primary crypto alternative. Selective inflows into XRP and Solana ETFs suggest some intra-crypto rotation into smaller tokens, but the amounts are marginal. The CME futures basis remained a small premium to spot — neither stressed nor euphoric. Net: broadly neutral.
### Policy / regulation — neutral (structural)
No crypto-specific regulatory catalyst landed inside the window. The medium-term regulatory backdrop in the US remains constructive (prior progress on market-structure legislation), which is a structural positive, but nothing in the window moved price. Net: neutral for the horizon.
### Macro / liquidity / FX — price-negative (cyclical, see §14)
The Dollar Index pushed to a six-week high near 99.3 after Federal Reserve minutes revealed a hawkish tilt, with markets increasingly pricing a 25-basis-point rate hike by year-end. A firmer dollar and a higher implied-rate path tighten conditions and are a direct headwind for non-yielding Bitcoin. Net: price-negative.
### Near-term catalysts — watch item
**The US PCE inflation release on 28 May 2026** is the highest-impact scheduled event inside the five-day horizon (prior reading 2.40% year-on-year). It is flagged as the primary watch item in §13d, §15 and §16. A hot print would extend the dollar's strength and the rate-hike narrative — additionally price-negative for Bitcoin; a soft print would relieve dollar pressure and could trigger a relief bounce. US markets are closed Monday 25 May for Memorial Day, so the first full-liquidity weekday session of the horizon is 26 May.
