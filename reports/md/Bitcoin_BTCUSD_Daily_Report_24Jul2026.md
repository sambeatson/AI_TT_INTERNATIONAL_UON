# §1 Executive Snapshot
Bitcoin enters the 24 July session with a consensus spot reference of approximately USD 65,800 per BTC, having recovered roughly 13 per cent from the 1 July low near 58,082 while stalling beneath a well-defined 66,000–67,000 supply band that has capped every rally attempt since the late-June breakdown. Third, a rising oil complex and a firm dollar are re-introducing an inflation and financial-conditions headwind that argues against a clean directional breakout. The short-term technical structure is constructive but flattening — a sequence of higher session lows since 17 July with a mildly negative five-session net change of −331 dollars — while the medium-term 25-session structure is a maturing uptrend, up 4,852 dollars from the base. That contradiction is not resolved silently and is carried through to the forward view. The single most important watch item is the 66,194 weekly first resistance and the round-number 66,000 shelf immediately above it: a daily close through that zone converts the current chop into a confirmed continuation, and failure there leaves the 64,020 weekly pivot as the pivot of record.

# §5 Consensus Build Explanation
Normalisation was straightforward for this asset because every admitted observation is already denominated in USD per whole BTC with no contract-size, delivery-location or currency conversion to reconcile. The work was therefore in venue selection and timestamp alignment rather than in unit translation.
Observations were weighted on three criteria: venue regulation, index construction, and timestamp proximity to the 23 July reference. The CoinDesk index and the Coinbase regulated-venue read carry the highest weight because the former is a multi-venue volume-weighted construct designed specifically to damp single-exchange distortion, and the latter is the designated primary venue for this instrument. Single-venue quotes from Bitfinex were down-weighted: Bitfinex historically trades at a small persistent premium or discount to the US regulated complex depending on funding conditions, and its 23 July print of 65,566 sits materially below the same-day Coinbase area read, which is consistent with that known basis rather than with a genuine price disagreement.
Two exclusions were made. The CryptoTicker 66,267 print was excluded from the median because it was captured during the intraday spike on the CLARITY Act headline and reflects a transient liquidity event rather than a settled level — including it would drag the consensus above the resistance band the market has not actually cleared. The CoinCodex 64,640 live tile was likewise down-weighted because it lagged the 22 July cluster by more than a full session without a corresponding market event to explain the 1,100-dollar gap, which points to a stale or slow-refreshing tile rather than a real print.
The resulting figure of 65,800 is the most defensible because it sits inside the tight 22 July three-source cluster, is consistent with the independently reported fact that Bitcoin held above 66,000 on 22 July before easing, and does not assume a breakout through a resistance band that all technical evidence says remains intact. The published range of 65,700–65,950 is deliberately narrow and honest about what the evidence supports; it is not a confidence interval on a model, it is the observed dispersion of the admitted sources.

# §9 Medium-Term Regime & Volatility
Regime classification over the 25-session block: TREND_UP, maturing and losing efficiency.
The structural case for the uptrend is quantitative. Close-to-close change across the block is +4,852 dollars, from 60,232 on 29 June to 64,680 on 19 July, an advance of roughly 8.1 per cent from the 1 July basing low of 58,082. Session lows have made a rising sequence since 1 July, and the 25-session low of 58,082 has not been challenged in fifteen sessions. Structure is intact and the direction of the structure is up.
Volatility read: Average True Range over fourteen sessions stands at 1,548 dollars against a 25-session mean of 1,696, and the five-session slope is −201 — volatility is compressing and is now roughly nine per cent below its own recent average. In a maturing uptrend, compressing volatility is a mildly supportive condition: it typically indicates orderly absorption rather than the expanding-range distribution that characterises a topping process. It also means the market is coiled, and compressed volatility resolves in whichever direction the eventual break takes, so it supplies no directional edge on its own.
**Kaufman confirmation — contradiction flagged.** The Kaufman efficiency ratio over ten sessions reads 0.19, materially below the trend boundary, which classifies the market as RANGE. This directly contradicts the TREND_UP structural label above and the contradiction is stated rather than resolved silently. The two readings are measuring different things and both are correct: the structure has genuinely travelled upward over the block, while the efficiency ratio says that travel was purchased with a great deal of offsetting movement rather than delivered in a clean directional impulse. On the question of which takes precedence for execution, the efficiency reading governs. A market covering ground at 0.19 efficiency does not reward trend-following entries at the top of its range, because the persistence that a breakout entry depends on is precisely what the ratio says is absent. The structural uptrend governs the bias; the efficiency ratio governs the execution. That distinction drives the strategy fork in §21.

# §12 Key Market Considerations
## Supply — neutral, mildly price-supportive at the margin
Structural. Issuance is fixed by protocol and unchanged. The material supply variable is behavioural rather than mechanical: institutional holders have continued to increase their share of long-term-holder supply even through the price decline, which withdraws float from the tradable market. Working against this, corporate treasury demand has visibly deteriorated — a leading treasury company has paused purchases to preserve liquidity and sold equity to raise 466.7 million dollars in early July, and a second treasury vehicle has seen its shareholders vote to return capital and cancel its listing. Treasury accumulation as a supply sink is materially weaker than a year ago.
## Demand — price-supportive, decelerating
Cyclical. US spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded a seventh consecutive session of net inflows on 22 July at 69.1 million dollars, taking July to roughly 699.2 million and making it the first net-inflow month since April. The concentration and the trajectory both matter: BlackRock's fund took 38.8 million of the 22 July total and had taken 80.7 per cent of the 21 July total, so the recovery is narrow. The daily pace has fallen from 226.9 million on 20 July to 203.1 million on 21 July to 69.1 million on 22 July — a two-thirds decline across three sessions. Set against roughly 4.51 billion of June outflows, July has recovered only about fifteen per cent of the prior month's damage, and 2026 remains net negative by roughly 4.76 billion. The direction of flow is supportive; the second derivative is not.
## Policy and regulation — price-supportive, the dominant discretionary catalyst
Cyclical, with structural consequences if it passes. The CLARITY Act would divide digital-asset oversight between the securities and commodities regulators, impose disclosure requirements on certain tokens, and extend anti-money-laundering and sanctions obligations to exchanges. The Treasury Secretary publicly described the bill as being at the one-yard line and urged passage before the congressional recess, a remark that moved Bitcoin above 67,000 intraday and lifted the principal listed exchange more than twelve per cent in a single session. Senate Republicans have since circulated a revised draft containing an ethics provision barring senior officials, including the President, from sponsoring crypto for compensation until January 2029 — the provision that had been the sticking point. The House passed its version a year ago. The residual risk is procedural rather than substantive: the bill still requires Senate floor time and bipartisan support, and the recess is the binding constraint.
## Macro and financial conditions — price-negative
Cyclical. The oil complex has re-accelerated on an eleventh consecutive night of US strikes on Iranian targets, with Brent settling around 94 dollars and WTI at 86.83 on 22 July, both at roughly six-week highs. Rising energy prices feed directly into headline inflation and therefore into rate expectations, and markets are pricing better than a fifty-five per cent probability of a rate rise in September. The dollar index has advanced for four straight sessions and holds above 101. Each of these channels tightens financial conditions for a non-yielding asset.
## Near-term catalysts — mixed, event-dense
- CLARITY Act Senate floor action before the recess — the single highest-impact item in the window.
- Federal Open Market Committee decision on 29 July, with rates broadly expected to be held and the committee currently in its communications blackout.
- Continued US-Iran escalation, transmitting through oil to inflation expectations and to broad risk appetite.
- Daily ETF flow prints, where the deceleration trend now matters more than the sign.
