**§12 Key Market Considerations**
**Supply**
Circulating supply ≈ 20.02m BTC of a 21m hard cap. Issuance is post-fourth-halving (≈ 3.125 BTC per block) and the next halving is more than two years out — issuance is not a near-term price driver. Exchange-held reserves continue the multi-month decline reported through Q1 2026, reducing immediate sellable float.
**Demand**
Spot Bitcoin ETF flows have been the dominant marginal-demand channel through 2026 and remain net positive on a rolling basis, though daily prints have softened during the CPI week as duration assets repriced. Corporate-treasury accumulation continues to be reported by mid-cap balance-sheet buyers. Retail demand — proxied by stablecoin issuance and on-exchange deposits — is moderate, not frothy.
**Policy**
US: the CLARITY Act markup is in progress this week. A clean read-through favouring spot crypto would be incrementally supportive; the more market-relevant question is whether the legislative language treats ETH classification cleanly (a current uncertainty). Federal Reserve: post-CPI, markets have priced out further 2026 cuts, and OIS pricing has moved to ~35% probability of a Q4 hike. This is a clear hawkish step from where pricing sat a month ago and is the dominant macro headwind for BTC.
**Macro**
Inflation surprise to the upside drives a tighter-for-longer narrative: real yields up, USD up, duration assets pressured. Bitcoin's response — holding the USD 80,000 shelf and producing a wide-range reversal bar — is constructive given the macro backdrop. Crude oil at USD 101+, lifted by Middle-East tensions, keeps headline inflation sticky. The Trump–Xi Beijing meeting (14–15 May) is a tail-risk event for trade-policy headlines but is unlikely to dominate BTC pricing unless the readout addresses crypto policy directly.
**Idiosyncratic catalysts**
ETH Glamsterdam upgrade trajectory remains a Q2 2026 watch item. Whale accumulation in ETH (140k+ ETH in early May per on-chain reads) is a notable internal-market signal; for BTC specifically the analogous signal is the persistent decline in exchange reserves.
