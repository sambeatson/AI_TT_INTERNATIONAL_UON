**§5 Consensus Build Explanation**
All eight observations were already denominated in USD per whole coin, so no unit or currency normalisation was required. Normalisation was therefore limited to timestamp alignment: every admitted observation falls inside a twenty-hour window ending 25 July 18:00 UTC, and Bitcoin traded a 1,706-dollar band across that window, so the dispersion between sources reflects venue and timing rather than any methodological difference.
Weighting followed the venue-quality hierarchy rather than a simple average. Direct quotes from regulated USD-pair venues carried full weight. The CoinDesk index and the two broad aggregators carried three-quarter weight, because their construction admits stablecoin-pair liquidity that is not part of the stated price basis. The contract-for-difference composite carried half weight, since its print reflects broker pricing rather than underlying exchange execution.
Two exclusions were made and are recorded here rather than resolved silently. The Investing.com print of 64,703.90 at 17:06 UTC sat 448 dollars above the next-highest observation and 572 dollars above the median, roughly 0.31 average-true-range units, with no corroborating move on any other feed inside the same window; it was treated as a stale or broker-specific print and excluded, with the 16:04 reading from the same source retained in its place. The narrative range quoted by the market-commentary source was excluded from the median entirely and used only to bound the observed 24-hour range, because it is a rounded editorial figure, not a quote.
The weighted median of the seven admitted observations resolves to 64,132.00. That number is the most defensible available because it is an actual executable quote from a regulated USD-pair venue rather than a constructed average, it sits within 118 dollars of the CoinDesk index and within 26 dollars of the second regulated venue, and it is insensitive to the two exclusions — removing them entirely moves the median by less than twenty dollars.

**§8 Short-Term Technical Analysis**

- 21 July — open 65,212, close 66,552, range 1,747. The strongest session of the block, a 1,340-dollar body with a close within 375 of the high. RSI2 finished at 96.16, an extreme reading. This session printed the 25-session high at 66,927 and marks the fib swing high referenced in §11.
- 22 July — open 66,617, close 66,063, range 1,148. Character change. The session opened above the prior close, failed to extend beyond 66,729, and closed 554 lower. Range contracted by a third. This is the first distribution session of the sequence.

Sequence assessment. The block is a completed round trip rather than a directional move: net change from the first open to the last close is minus 552 dollars, or minus 0.85%, on a block range of 3,099 dollars — 1.69 average-true-range units. Session ranges have oscillated without trending (1,925 / 1,747 / 1,148 / 1,604 / 1,301), and the overlap between consecutive sessions is heavy throughout. Highs have descended in an orderly line since 21 July — 66,927, 66,729, 66,276, 65,247 — which is the defining bearish feature; but lows have not confirmed, with 24 July's low at 63,946 still above the block floor at 63,828. That divergence is the whole argument of the short-term picture: sellers control the upper boundary, buyers have so far held the lower one.
Short-term classification: Ranging — Mixed. Two-period RSI at 12.0 is a stretched reading. Inside a ranging structure that reading has historically resolved by stabilisation more often than by continuation, and it argues against initiating fresh downside exposure at the current level without a confirmed break of 63,828.
**§9 Medium-Term Regime & VOLator**
Regime classification. Across the 25 sessions from 30 June to 24 July, Bitcoin closed 5,453 dollars, or 9.3%, above where the block began. That headline conceals the shape, which is what governs the classification. The advance came almost entirely in two bursts — 1 to 6 July (58,631 to 64,116) and 20 to 21 July (64,680 to 66,552) — separated by roughly ten sessions of overlapping, directionless trade between 62,300 and 65,000. Directional persistence is therefore weak even though net travel is positive. The block range is 58,082 to 66,927, a width of 8,845 dollars or 4.82 average-true-range units, and price closed the block at 68% of that range.
The structural feature that does hold is the sequence of rising session lows: 58,082 on 1 July, then 61,452, 61,846, 62,550 and most recently 63,828 on 20 July. None of those has been broken. Medium-term classification is therefore Ranging — Bullish: an intact upward bias operating inside a wide range rather than a trend.
Volatility read. Average true range over fourteen sessions stands at 1,834 dollars, or 2.82% of price, against a 25-session mean of 3.88%. Expressed as a historical z-score and clipped to the ±1 band, the scaled reading has sat at the lower bound of minus 1.0 for six consecutive sessions, with a slope of zero. This is unambiguous compression: realised volatility is at the floor of its own recent distribution and is no longer falling. Compression of this depth and duration is not directional information in itself, but it is a reliable precursor to range expansion, and the expansion catalyst is already on the calendar (§13).
Efficiency-ratio confirmation. The smoothed thirteen-session efficiency ratio reads plus 0.102, classified as Ranging — Upward Bias. It fell from 0.277 on 22 July, having briefly crossed into Trending Up — Strong territory during the 21 July thrust. The current reading agrees with the ranging synthesis on both timeframes and, importantly, denies a trending classification in either direction. Where the ratio and the structural read might have diverged — the medium-term higher lows could support a trending-up label — the efficiency ratio takes precedence, because directional travel that requires 4.82 range-units of path to produce 2.9 range-units of net movement is definitionally inefficient. The consolidated regime is RANGE, and both required gates are satisfied: absolute efficiency of 0.102 is below the 0.13 trending threshold, and the volatility slope is zero, that is, not positive.

**§12 Key Market Considerations**
**Supply.**
- Issuance is fixed and non-responsive to price; the block subsidy stands at 3.125 coins. The relevant supply variable is therefore holder behaviour, not production.
- Miner economics have deteriorated materially. Hash-price fell roughly 15% across June against a 7.15% difficulty increase late in that month, and one mining pool operator filed for Chapter 11 protection on 24 July. Distressed miner selling is a live, if second-order, supply channel.
- On-chain distribution shows accumulation, not distribution, at the large-holder tier: wallets holding between one thousand and ten thousand coins continued to add through the 23–24 July weakness.
**Demand.**
- The dominant demand channel remains US spot exchange-traded funds. That channel turned negative on 23 July with roughly 225 million dollars of net redemptions, ending a seven-session run that had drawn in close to one billion. A single large fund accounted for approximately 202 million of the outflow.
- Weekly fund trading volume fell to its lowest full-week level since October 2024, which indicates disengagement rather than active selling — a materially different condition from a distribution event.
- Ether funds moved the other way, extending an inflow streak to a fifth session with 26.3 million dollars added. Within-asset-class rotation is therefore occurring alongside, and partly disguising, the Bitcoin outflow.
**Policy and regulation.**
- The CLARITY Act remains in legislative deadlock. Falling odds of passage were cited alongside the ETF outflows as a proximate cause of the 24 July weakness.
- Structural adoption continues independently of price. A large asset manager has softened its stance on digital assets, the SEC approved a Solana exchange-traded product for a major US bank, and Russia's largest bank has announced a December 2026 target for crypto trading and custody infrastructure.
- Housing-finance regulators have directed the two government-sponsored mortgage enterprises to prepare to count cryptocurrency as a qualifying asset — a slow-burn but genuine widening of the collateral base.
**Macro and catalysts.**
- A new tariff schedule of 10% and 12.5% applies to sixty trading partners, replacing an expiring universal 10% rate. The inflation pass-through is the transmission channel that matters here.
- Middle East escalation drove Brent through 100 dollars mid-week before a 4% retreat on Friday, on reports of a possible third-party-mediated path to US–Iran negotiations.
- The Federal Open Market Committee meets on Wednesday 29 July. No rate change is anticipated; the statement language is the catalyst.
