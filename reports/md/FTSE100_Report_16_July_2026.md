# §5 Consensus Build Explanation
Normalisation. All admitted quotes are already denominated in index points on the same basis — the London Stock Exchange cash close of the FTSE 100 — so no currency, contract-month, or location normalisation was required. The only transformation applied was decimal alignment: Reuters reports to one decimal place (10,515.9) and Alliance News to two (10,515.92). These are the same observation at different precision, not two competing observations.
Weighting. The stated consensus method for this instance is a weighted median. In this case the two highest-weighted observations agree exactly, so the weighted median collapses to the reported official close and no dispersion calculation was meaningful. Tier-1 wire reports of an official index close were weighted above aggregator data, and aggregator data above derived or CFD-referenced quotes, which were excluded outright.
Exclusions. One source (Sunday Guardian) reported 10,498.81 and −0.29% for the same session. This conflicts with two independent Tier-1 primaries on both the level and the direction magnitude, and its timestamp (17:21 IST, equivalent to 12:51 UK) places it well before the 16:30 UK close. It is therefore an intraday capture mislabelled as a session result, and was excluded rather than blended. Blending it would have dragged the consensus roughly 17 points below the corroborated official close for no evidentiary gain.
Why this number is defensible. 10,515.92 is the value reported by the exchange-facing wire services, is internally consistent with the reported point change (−13.47 from the 14 July close of 10,529.39, which itself is dual-source corroborated), and reconciles arithmetically: 10,529.39 − 13.47 = 10,515.92. That arithmetic closure across two separate sessions and two separate source sets is the strongest available evidence.

# §8 Short-Term Technical Analysis
Candle-by-candle. Thursday 9 July opened at the prior close of 10,489.04 and sold to 10,420.00 before closing at 10,465.00 — a down session that extended the 8 July collapse (a −1.7% day driven by the initial Middle East escalation) but at roughly half the range, the first sign that the selling was losing force. Friday 10 July reversed: the index opened at 10,465.00, held a higher low at 10,440.00, and closed at 10,496.90, recovering the bulk of the previous session. Monday 13 July was the narrowest session of the window — a 50-point range and a close of 10,496.00, effectively unchanged from Friday. That inside-day compression is the pivot of the whole sequence.
Tuesday 14 July is the most informative candle in the window and the only fully corroborated one. It opened at 10,470.08, sold immediately to 10,422.98 in a choppy opening two hours on US–Iran risk aversion, then rallied steadily through the afternoon to a session high of 10,552.56 and closed at 10,529.39 — a gain of 31.10 points, or 0.30%, and a recovery of nearly 130 points from the intraday low. The candle is a long-tailed bullish reversal: buyers absorbed a full retest of the 9 July low region and closed the index in the upper quarter of the day's range. London outperformed every major continental bourse that session (see §6).
Wednesday 15 July failed to extend. The index opened near 10,510, traded a 65-point range, and closed at 10,515.92, down 13.47 points, snapping a three-day winning streak. Critically, it did not retrace the 14 July gain — it stalled. Precious-metal miners fell 2.7% and industrial miners 2.1% on the China GDP miss, while energy dipped 0.9% tracking crude. The session was a sector-rotation standoff, not a directional statement.
Sequence assessment. The five-day sequence is: capitulation-aftermath (9 Jul) → recovery (10 Jul) → compression (13 Jul) → expansion-reversal (14 Jul) → stall (15 Jul). Net travel is +50.92 points across five sessions on a cumulative true range of roughly 415 points — the index has moved a small fraction of the distance it actually travelled. This is the signature of a transitional tape: real two-way flow, no sustained directional commitment. Lows are rising (10,420.00 → 10,440.00 → 10,470.00 → 10,422.98 → 10,480.00) with one violation on the 14th that was immediately rejected. Highs are broadly flat in the 10,510–10,553 band. That is a market coiling beneath resistance rather than trending into it.
RSI2 reads 55.3 at the 15 July close — squarely mid-range, neither overbought nor oversold, and consistent with the stall read. The RSI2 spike to 86.5 on 14 July confirmed the reversal candle but has already unwound, which removes the short-term overbought objection to further upside without itself supplying a buy signal.
# §9 Medium-Term Regime & VOLator
Regime classification: TRANSITION. The available medium-term evidence does not support a trending label in either direction. The index traded a record zone near 10,930 in late February, spent March materially lower near 10,090, rebuilt through May and June in a 10,230–10,570 band, and now sits at 10,515.92 — within that same band, at its upper portion. Across the assembled window the structure is a wide, rotational range with heavy session overlap and no persistent directional drift. The February record high remains roughly 4% above the current level and has not been challenged in over four months.
The 8 July selloff (−1.7%) broke the June rising structure and the subsequent four sessions have rebuilt most of that loss without reclaiming the highs. That is the textbook definition of a transition: the prior structure has been broken and tested, and the next confirmed break carries elevated follow-through probability precisely because the range has already been faked out.
VOLator read. Average true range across the six-session window is approximately 104 points. The sequence — 210 → 100 → 70 → 50 → 130 → 65 — shows compression from the 8 July shock into the 13 July inside day, a single-session expansion on the 14th, and re-compression on the 15th. Scaled against its own window history the FTSE 100 volatility series ends near the flatline, having spiked and immediately mean-reverted. Compression, not expansion, is the current state, and compression into a resistance shelf typically resolves with a directional break rather than a drift. The scaled series ends below the counters, indicating the FTSE 100 is currently the calmer instrument in its peer set.
Regime and VOLator agreement. The two agree. A transitional structure with compressing volatility beneath defined resistance is a coherent, internally consistent read — it is a market waiting for a catalyst, not a market in conflict with itself.
Kaufman confirmation. The Kaufman efficiency ratio reads 0.26 on the available window, against a trending threshold of 0.13. Read literally this classifies the tape as TRENDING and therefore contradicts the transitional label derived from structure. Two considerations govern the resolution. First, the reading is computed on a six-session window rather than the specified thirteen-session lookback, because corroborated data for the intervening period could not be assembled — it is mechanically an efficiency measure of the post-selloff recovery leg in isolation, which will always read efficient. Second, the structural evidence (four months without challenging the highs, heavy overlap, a broken-then-rebuilt June structure) is broad and unambiguous where the efficiency reading is narrow and window-limited. Structure takes precedence: the regime label stands at TRANSITION. The Kaufman reading is retained in the direction score at its specified weight rather than discarded, so its bullish contribution is preserved without allowing it to overturn the regime.

# §12 Key Market Considerations
## Supply and demand — sector composition
The FTSE 100's defining feature this week is that its sector composition is doing the work. Energy (Shell, BP) carries a heavy index weight and is being supported by crude strength driven by the Hormuz threat. BP rose 2.2% on 14 July after signalling a stronger-than-usual second-quarter oil trading result, building on an already exceptionally strong first quarter. Financials — Barclays, HSBC, Lloyds — are being lifted by the read-across from US bank earnings. Against that, mining is being hit hard: precious-metal miners fell 2.7% and industrial miners 2.1% on 15 July, with Anglo American −3.4% and Glencore −2.4%. The index is not moving because these forces are cancelling.
## Policy
The June US CPI print came in below expectations, materially reviving expectations of monetary easing and driving the 14 July recovery in both US and UK financials. This is the dominant policy input in the window. Neither a Bank of England nor an ECB decision falls in the immediate forward horizon on the available calendar.
## Macro
China's Q2 GDP expanded 4.3% year-on-year, slowing from 5.0% in Q1 and missing consensus of 4.5% — the weakest pace since 2022. Quarter-on-quarter growth of 0.9% marked the slowest expansion in more than two years. This is the proximate cause of Wednesday's mining weakness and is a live, unresolved drag on the resources complex.
## Catalysts
US Q2 earnings season began 14 July with the major Wall Street banks. Goldman Sachs posted earnings of $20.98 per share against a consensus of $14.48, on revenue of $20.34 billion versus $16.13 billion expected — a very large beat. Morgan Stanley reported a jump in Q2 earnings on 15 July driven by equities revenue and investment banking momentum. Given the FTSE 100's financials weight, the earnings read-across is a genuine transmission channel and remains active through the coming sessions.
## Geopolitics
US Central Command began a wave of strikes against Iran at approximately 06:00 ET on 15 July, targeting military capabilities used against commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz. Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps stated that regional energy exports would be either shared by all or denied to all, and that the Strait would remain closed indefinitely. Notably, crude fell roughly 1% on the day despite this — Brent traded under $84 — which suggests the market is currently discounting the closure threat rather than pricing it. That discount is the single largest tail risk in this analysis.
