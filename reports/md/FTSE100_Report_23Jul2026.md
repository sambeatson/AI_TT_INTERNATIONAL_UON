# §5 Consensus Build Explanation
All sources quote the same instrument on the same basis — the FTSE 100 cash index at the London Stock Exchange close — so no cross-venue or cross-currency normalisation was required for the primary asset. The EURO STOXX 50 reference is reported in its native euro denomination rather than converted, because the instance-level sterling conversion adds a currency-translation error term that obscures the underlying index move; the sterling-euro cross is instead handled explicitly in the macro and cross-asset sections.
Weighting followed the weighted-median method. Exchange-derived closes carried by Alliance News and Share Talk received full weight; institutional newswire percentage reports received full weight for direction and partial weight for level, since a percentage move reconciles to a level only through the prior close; aggregator quotes received third-tier weight. The Trading Economics figure of 10,662 was excluded from the consensus build entirely. It is a contract-for-difference tracker rather than the cash index, and the instance restrictions exclude retail CFD quotes on the grounds that broker spreads mislead. Its 55-point divergence from the cash close is precisely the artefact those restrictions anticipate, and admitting it would have dragged the weighted median below the true settlement.
The final figure of 10,716.97 is the most defensible number available because it is the administrator-published cash close, it is independently reproduced by two Tier 1 sources to the second decimal place, and it reconciles arithmetically with the prior validated close of 10,585.91 plus the reported change of +131.06 points. That internal arithmetic check is the strongest available evidence in the absence of a direct FTSE Russell feed.

# §12 Key Market Considerations
**Monetary policy — price-supportive (cyclical)**
UK annual inflation slowed to 2.6% in June from 2.8% in May against a 2.7% consensus, the lowest reading since March 2025, driven by slower motor fuel and food price growth (Office for National Statistics). The mechanism is direct: a softer print weakens the case for further Bank of England tightening, which lowers the discount rate applied to UK equity cash flows and reduces the funding-cost drag on the index's leveraged constituents. This is cyclical and reversible — the same oil complex now supporting the energy majors feeds into the July and August fuel components with a lag.
**Energy — price-supportive on balance, structurally two-sided (cyclical)**
Brent crude rose above US$95.43 a barrel on 22 July, extending gains for a fourth consecutive session, after US strikes on Iranian targets entered an eleventh consecutive night and Houthi forces disrupted Red Sea shipping. Shell gained 0.5% and BP 1.0% on the session. For an index with the FTSE's energy weighting the first-order effect is clearly positive. The second-order effect is negative and slower-acting: sustained crude above US$95 reverses the disinflation that drove the 22 July advance, raises input costs for the industrial and consumer constituents, and lifts freight costs across the retail complex.
**Fiscal and political — price-supportive (cyclical, with structural potential)**
Andy Burnham took office as Prime Minister on 20 July, appointing John Healey as Chancellor in a move markets did not anticipate. Healey's commitment to fiscal discipline was the proximate driver of the 21 July advance, and his defence background drove the sector rally that led that session — Babcock rose 4.14%. Burnham announced an £850 million tax cut on electricity bills as the first act of a stated cost-of-living agenda. The ten-year gilt near 5.03% is the constraint on how far this can run; a fiscal loosening that pushes gilt yields higher would reverse the equity-positive read quickly.
**Sector composition — price-supportive (structural)**
The index's limited semiconductor and artificial-intelligence capital-expenditure exposure has become an active advantage during the global technology rotation. This is a structural characteristic of the FTSE 100 rather than an event, and it has persisted through several weeks of technology weakness. It is the single most durable support in this list.
**Corporate activity — price-supportive (cyclical)**
Airbus rose more than 6% on a €5 billion buyback and raised guidance; Fresnillo reaffirmed 2026–2028 production guidance; Mitie announced a £3.1 billion acquisition. Against this, JD Wetherspoon issued a profit warning and London Stock Exchange Group fell 2.4% on its overnight-trading venue announcement. Net effect is positive but the dispersion is widening.

- US weekly jobless claims, 23 July — secondary, transmits through the S&P 500 counter
