**§8 SHORT-TERM TECHNICAL ANALYSIS**
**Session-by-session commentary (see §6)**
Friday 17 July. The index opened at 10,620.15, reached 10,668.42, and settled at 10,598.91 — a 21-point open-to-close loss on an 82-point range. The upper shadow of roughly 70 points relative to a modest body marks this as a distribution session at the upper end of the then-prevailing range. Volatility was the narrowest of the window.
Monday 20 July. The heaviest directional session of the first half of the window. The index opened near the prior close at 10,596.44 and fell steadily to 10,499.18 before closing at 10,524.76, down 74.15 points or 0.70%. The true range of 111.84 points was a 36% expansion on Friday. RSI2 collapsed to 0.0 — a fully saturated short-term oversold reading. The proximate driver was the UK political transition and the appointment of a new Chancellor, compounded by global technology weakness and Middle East escalation. Critically, the low of 10,499.18 held above the prior structural low, preserving the higher-lows sequence.
Tuesday 21 July. A recovery inside day. The index gapped marginally lower to open at 10,524.25, probed to 10,483.14 — below Monday's low — and then reversed to close at 10,585.91, up 61.15 points or 0.58%. This is the most technically significant candle of the window: a failed downside extension followed by a close in the upper quartile of the range. Defence shares led. The 10,483.14 low is the anchor of the swing used for retracement work in §21 and remains the nearest structural floor.
Wednesday 22 July. The dominant session. Softer-than-expected UK June inflation was released, the index opened effectively unchanged at 10,585.87, and rallied without meaningful interruption to 10,763.44 — a three-month high — before easing to close at 10,716.97, up 131.06 points or 1.24%. The true range of 194.72 points was 78% above the four-session mean and the widest of the window. RSI2 reached 81.3. London was the strongest of the major Western European benchmarks that session, a notable divergence given that the eurozone comparator rose only 0.96%. The close in the upper quartile of a wide expanding range is a textbook trend-continuation signature.
Thursday 23 July. The reversal. The index opened at 10,716.99, effectively flat on the prior close, extended only 7.86 points to 10,724.85, and then declined through the session to close at 10,639.00, down 77.97 points or 0.73%. The 112.45-point true range was a contraction on Wednesday but the distribution within it is unfavourable: the body occupies the lower third and the failure to take out Wednesday's high by any meaningful margin, despite opening at it, constitutes a rejection of the 10,720–10,765 zone. RSI2 fell back to 45.6, a neutral reading that has unwound the overbought condition without generating a fresh oversold signal. Drivers were the European Central Bank's hawkish hold, rising bond yields, Brent's approach to $100, and renewed Middle East escalation.

**Sequence assessment**
The five-session sequence is: distribution → capitulation → reversal → expansion → rejection. Net change across the window is +40.09 points, or +0.38% — the index has travelled a great deal to end approximately where it started. Two of the five sessions closed in the upper quartile of their range and two in the lower third, with one neutral. The window's structural low is 10,483.14 (21 July) and its high 10,763.44 (22 July), giving a 280.30-point swing that is 2.37 times the current 14-session average true range and therefore a valid retracement anchor without adaptive extension.
The critical inference is that Wednesday's expansion was not confirmed by Thursday. In a healthy continuation the session following a 194-point expansion day either extends or consolidates in the upper half; instead the market opened at the prior close and sold off. That is a failure of follow-through, not a trend break — the higher-lows sequence remains intact and Thursday's low of 10,612.40 is comfortably above Tuesday's 10,483.14. The short-term technical bias is therefore mildly negative, scored at -0.30, and this reading is consistent with but not identical to the medium-term regime in §9, which remains constructive. The judgement label for the short-term block is REJECTION WITHIN UPTREND.

**Volatility read**
The 14-session average true range stands at 118.40 index points against a nine-period historical mean of approximately 106.0 points. The scaled deviation is +1.00 after clipping, placing the volatility regime at the upper bound of its historical band: EXPANDING. The slope of the volatility series is positive across the last four observations.
The direction of the expansion matters more than its magnitude. Volatility expanded into Wednesday's advance, which is benign — rising volatility on a directional up-move is characteristic of genuine participation. It then remained elevated through Thursday's decline, which is not benign. Sustained high volatility across a directional reversal indicates two-way conviction rather than an orderly pullback, and it materially widens the distribution of outcomes for the next session. The practical consequence for execution is that structural stops must sit outside the noise band: a 0.15 average-true-range buffer is approximately 17.8 points, and any stop placed inside that distance from a structural level should be treated as exposed.

**Kaufman efficiency confirmation**
The 13-session Kaufman Efficiency Ratio, smoothed with a 3-period exponential average, reads +0.5051. The trending threshold is 0.13 and the ranging-bias inner band is 0.09. At 0.51 the reading is nearly four times the trending threshold, classifying the market as TRENDING with strongly positive directional efficiency — the index has converted roughly 51% of its total 13-session path length into net directional progress, which is a high figure for an equity index.
**Contradiction flag.** The efficiency classification (TRENDING, upward) contradicts the short-term technical bias in §8 (REJECTION, mildly negative). This contradiction is not resolved silently. The efficiency ratio takes precedence for regime classification because it measures a 13-session window and is by construction insensitive to a single-session reversal; the short-term bias takes precedence for execution timing because it measures the two sessions that actually determine where the next open prints. The practical synthesis is that the medium-term structure supports buying weakness rather than selling strength, but the immediate session carries downside risk that must be respected through entry placement rather than through directional selection. This contradiction is carried forward to §15 and §16 and is the principal reason directional conviction in §21a resolves to NEUTRAL rather than LONG.
