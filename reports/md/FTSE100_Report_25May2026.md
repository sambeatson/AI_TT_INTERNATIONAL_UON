**Demand / risk appetite —** **Price-supportive** *[Cyclical]*
Equity-index ‘demand’ is risk appetite for UK large-caps. The four-week losing streak that ended on 22 May had compressed positioning; the de-escalation rally drew buyers back across financials, miners and energy. Improved UK consumer confidence in May (a modest beat) is a marginal positive. The FTSE’s ~80% multinational revenue base means global growth expectations matter as much as the UK domestic picture.
**Energy and commodities —** **Mixed / two-sided** *[Cyclical]*
The FTSE carries a heavy energy weighting via Shell and BP. Brent near $100 supports energy-sector earnings but, sustained, feeds UK inflation and complicates the rate outlook. On 22 May BP was among the largest single-stock laggards even as the index rose, illustrating that the energy tailwind is sector-specific and currently offset elsewhere. A genuine US–Iran de-escalation that pulls oil lower would be net-positive for the index by easing the inflation channel, despite trimming energy earnings.
**Policy and central banks —** **Price-supportive (near-term)** *[Cyclical]*
The Bank of England held Bank Rate at 3.75% on 29 April in an 8–1 vote, with one member favouring a hike. The week’s soft UK data — a retail-sales slump (−1.3% versus −0.6% expected) and higher public borrowing — reduced the market-implied odds of a near-term BoE hike, which is supportive for rate-sensitive UK equities. The European Central Bank backdrop is broadly stable. The direction of this factor is cyclical and could reverse quickly on a hot inflation print.
**Macro and FX —** **Neutral** *[Cyclical]*
Sterling moves are two-sided for the FTSE: GBP weakness lifts multinational earnings translation but can signal domestic stress. The Dollar Index is near-neutral. UK gilt yields have been elevated and politically sensitive amid pressure on the government — a watch item rather than an active driver this session.
**Substitution / relative value —** **Price-supportive** *[Structural]*
The FTSE 100 continues to trade at a valuation discount to the US and to several European peers, with a competitive dividend yield. That discount is a structural support for international allocation into UK large-caps and underpinned the exceptional 2025 performance; it does not, on its own, drive near-term direction.
**Near-term catalysts —** **Watch — direction pending** *[Cyclical]*
The dominant scheduled risk over the 5-day horizon is the evolving UK inflation picture and the approach to the 18 June Bank of England decision, alongside any hard news from the US–Iran negotiations. A hawkish data surprise or a breakdown in the talks are the two events most capable of overturning the constructive near-term view. These are carried into §13d, §15 and §16.
