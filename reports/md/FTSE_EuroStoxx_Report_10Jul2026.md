# §5 Consensus Build Explanation
Normalisation was straightforward: all six sources report the FTSE 100 in native GBP index points on the same LSE regular-session basis, so no currency or unit conversion was required. The only material normalisation issue is timestamp discipline. Three of the six quotes are intraday snapshots taken between 09:43 and 14:36 BST and therefore do not represent a close. These were classified as contextual and excluded from the numeric consensus rather than being time-adjusted, because time-adjusting an intraday print to an estimated close would constitute synthesis.
The weighted-median method was applied across the two sources returning a definitive settlement value. Both cluster at 10,429, with the difference attributable to one source publishing to the nearest whole point. The higher-precision value was retained. The one source reporting a −0.2% daily change against others reporting −0.57% was down-weighted to a directional-confirmation role only; the divergence most likely reflects a different reference previous-close rather than a data error, and resolving it would require inference.
Two sources were excluded from the numeric build entirely: one CFD-derived feed, per the standing restriction against retail contract-for-difference quotes whose spreads can distort the level; and one delayed-by-15-minutes exchange relay whose final timestamp preceded the close. The final number is defensible because it is the value on which two independent, non-CFD, close-basis sources converge within tolerance, and because every value entering the technical work below traces directly to a fetched quote rather than to a reconstruction.

# §8 Short-Term Technical Analysis
Candle-by-candle across the five-session block, the sequence is a clean three-stage failure. The 3 July candle is the block's structural high: a wide-bodied advance closing at 10,679.03 on a session high of 10,747.01, which is the swing high the block is measured from (see §6). The 6 July candle is an inside consolidation — a small-bodied retreat closing 27 points lower, entirely within the prior session's envelope, carrying no directional information on its own. The 7 July candle recovered to 10,691.98, the block's highest close, and at that point the sequence read as an orderly higher-low pullback within an uptrend.
The 8 July candle destroys that reading. It opens at 10,666.09 and never trades above the open — open equals high — then travels 199 points to a low of 10,467.01 and closes at 10,489.04, near the bottom of its own range. A candle whose open is its high and whose close sits in the lower quartile is a distribution candle: every buyer who transacted during the session was underwater at the close. The 9 July candle then gaps the open down to 10,441.09, some 48 points beneath the prior close, fails to reclaim any of that gap on a session high of only 10,458.95, and settles at 10,429.22 — the lowest close of the block, and beneath the prior session's low.
Sequence assessment: three consecutive lower closes, a lower high on each of the final two sessions, and a down-gap that was not filled. RSI2 has collapsed from 59.6 to 16.5 to 0.0 across three prints. A zero reading on a two-period oscillator is mechanically produced by consecutive down-closes with no intervening up-move, so it is a statement about the purity of the sequence rather than an independent signal. It is a genuinely oversold condition on the short horizon and it is exactly what a strong downtrend also looks like at its outset — the reading does not distinguish between the two, and treating an RSI2 floor as a mean-reversion trigger inside a trending regime is the classic misuse of the indicator. The five-session swing high stands at 10,747.01 and the swing low at 10,419.22, giving a block range of 327.79 points, of which 260 was traversed in the final two sessions.

# §12 Key Market Considerations
## Supply-side and sector composition
The FTSE 100's problem on 9 July was compositional, not macroeconomic. AstraZeneca, among the index's largest constituents by weight, fell approximately 9% after Wainua — its gene-silencing therapy developed with Ionis Pharmaceuticals — failed to meet its primary endpoint of reducing cardiovascular complications in a rare and potentially fatal cardiac disease. A single-stock move of that magnitude in a constituent of that weight is worth a material fraction of a percent on the index outright, and it fell on a day when the healthcare sector lagged across the continent. Energy compounded it: Shell fell roughly 0.8% and BP roughly 1.3% as crude reversed earlier gains despite the geopolitical backdrop, punishing the FTSE's heavy energy weighting on a day when that weighting should have been a hedge.
## Demand and flow
Mid-caps rebounded roughly 0.5% while large-caps fell, and mining names showed a split personality — Endeavour and Antofagasta dragged on one session and Antofagasta and Anglo American gained 3.6% and 3.0% on the next. Basic resources led the pan-European advance at +3.2%. The flow picture is of rotation into cyclicals and semiconductors and out of defensives and pharmaceuticals, which is precisely the wrong rotation for the FTSE's composition.
## Policy
The Bank of England and the European Central Bank both face a reconstituted inflation problem. Renewed Middle East escalation and the associated fuel-price surge have pushed sovereign yields higher on expectations that the ECB delivers further hikes this year. For the FTSE this is a two-sided consideration: higher rates compress equity multiples generally, but the index's substantial banking weight benefits from a steeper curve. UK data was quietly constructive — the RICS survey showed house-price declines continuing in June but at an easing pace, suggesting stabilisation.
## Macro and catalysts
The dominant catalyst is the collapse of the US-Iran ceasefire and confirmed US strikes on Iran for a second consecutive day, with Iran threatening large-scale retaliation against regional US bases. Shipping continues through the Strait of Hormuz but at below-normal volumes. Oil's behaviour is the tell: crude surged nearly 7% across two sessions and then fell back on 9 July, and it was that reversal — not the escalation — that hurt the FTSE's energy majors. A market that sells the energy complex on a day of active military escalation is a market that has stopped pricing the geopolitical premium and started pricing demand destruction.

# §14 Macro Context
Rates: sovereign yields rose across the eurozone as the fuel-price surge revived expectations that the ECB delivers additional hikes in 2026. Ten-year Bund and Gilt yields have moved with that repricing. For the FTSE this is directionally negative for the aggregate multiple and directionally positive for the banking complex, which is a substantial index weight — one of several reasons the index's sector composition produces genuinely ambiguous rate sensitivity rather than the clean inverse relationship a simpler index would show.
Inflation: the entire macro impulse of the last 48 hours runs through the oil channel. Crude's near-7% two-session surge, followed by a reversal on 9 July, has left the inflation expectation embedded in rates markets elevated while the equity-side energy hedge has already been given back. That asymmetry — rates still pricing the shock, equities no longer pricing the hedge — is the least comfortable feature of the current setup.
Liquidity and positioning: the pan-European rotation into semiconductors and basic resources and out of defensives, healthcare and consumer staples is well-established across at least two sessions. Investors are moving away from highly-valued momentum names toward stability in the US while doing the opposite in Europe. The FTSE sits on the wrong side of both trades.
FX: the dollar has been firm. Sterling weakness against a firm dollar is mechanically supportive of the FTSE 100 given the roughly 80% overseas revenue share of its constituents. This support was present on 9 July and the index fell anyway, which is itself diagnostic — it means the sector-composition drag was large enough to overwhelm the translation tailwind.
