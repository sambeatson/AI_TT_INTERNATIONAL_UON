# 12. Key Market Considerations

Structured fundamentals. Each factor carries a direction label and is tagged structural (6+ months) or cyclical (event-driven, reversible within the 5-day horizon).
### Energy & commodity exposure — price-supportive (structural)
Shell and BP are among the largest FTSE 100 constituents. With Brent crude holding near four-year highs above $104, energy majors are generating strong cash flows and funding buybacks and dividends, cushioning the index even on risk-off days. This is a structural support specific to the FTSE's sector composition and a key reason it has outperformed Continental peers at times in 2026.
### Geopolitics — US–Iran negotiations — cyclical, two-sided
The US–Iran track is the dominant cyclical catalyst. De-escalation headlines drove the 19–21 May relief rally; a breakdown that pushes Brent toward $110+ would lift energy names but hit transport, consumer and rate-sensitive sectors and revive inflation fears. The factor is genuinely two-sided and is the principal source of the Medium (not High) confidence rating.
### UK monetary policy & inflation — price-negative to neutral (cyclical)
UK CPI has run around 3.3% and risks pushing higher on energy pass-through. The Bank of England has held its policy rate, and markets have pared 2026 rate-cut expectations toward roughly two hikes priced by year-end. Higher-for-longer rates pressure valuations and domestically-focused names; a softer-than-expected recent CPI print was, however, part of the trigger for the rebound. Net: a mild headwind that briefly turned supportive.
### Sterling & gilts — mixed (structural / cyclical)
Sterling weakness and UK gilt-yield volatility — with the 10-year reportedly pressing toward 5% — reflect fiscal and political uncertainty around the Starmer government. For the FTSE 100 specifically this is double-edged: gilt stress and political noise weigh on sentiment and domestic stocks, but a weaker pound mechanically lifts the index's overseas-earnings translation.
### Substitution / cross-market — neutral
Versus the DAX 40, CAC 40 and S&P 500, the FTSE trades at a persistent valuation discount with a higher dividend yield — a structural pull factor for value-oriented allocation, but not an acute near-term driver.
### Near-term catalysts (cyclical) — see §13d
- Bank of England commentary / policy signals — the single highest-impact watch item for the week ahead.
- US–Iran negotiation headlines and any Strait of Hormuz development — direct read-through to Brent and energy weighting.
