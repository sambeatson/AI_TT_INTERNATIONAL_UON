# §12 Key Market Considerations
## Energy and commodities — currently price-supportive (cyclical)
The FTSE 100 carries a heavy energy and mining weight through Shell, BP, Rio Tinto, Glencore, Anglo American, Antofagasta and Fresnillo. Two forces are in play. First, the prior fortnight's US–Iran de-escalation hopes pulled Brent crude down sharply — from above 104 dollars on 22 May toward the high-90s — which is mildly negative for the oil majors but broadly positive for the wider index by easing the inflation impulse. Second, on 26 May fresh US strikes on Iran lifted Brent again to roughly 98 dollars; UK-listed miners led the index that session. The net effect is currently supportive because commodity strength is lifting the mining complex faster than energy softness is dragging the oil names. This is a cyclical, event-driven factor that could reverse quickly on any Middle East headline.
## UK monetary policy and rates — currently price-supportive (cyclical)
April UK retail sales fell roughly 1.3%, almost double the consensus decline, and public-sector borrowing came in above expectations. Together with the broader data run, this has reduced the market-implied probability that the Bank of England tightens further. A lower expected path of UK rates is supportive for equity valuations and for rate-sensitive domestic sectors. This factor is squarely cyclical and is directly exposed to the 28 May MPC decision flagged in §13d.
## Currency — currently price-supportive (structural-cyclical)
With about 80% of FTSE 100 constituent revenue earned abroad, sterling weakness mechanically lifts the GBP value of overseas earnings. Sterling was firm-to-mixed over the window (around 1.348 against the dollar at the 26 May midday point) so the currency channel is currently a mild tailwind rather than a strong one. The structural overseas-revenue tilt is a lasting feature of the index; the day-to-day currency move is cyclical.
## Geopolitics — currently a two-way risk (cyclical)
The US–Iran situation is the dominant swing factor. De-escalation lowers oil and supports the broad index; renewed strikes raise oil, support energy names but unsettle Continental risk appetite. The fragile ceasefire dated to early April remains the reference point, and headlines on the Strait of Hormuz are capable of moving the index intraday in either direction.
## Near-term catalysts
- **Thursday 28 May — Bank of England MPC decision and statement.** The single highest-impact event for the FTSE 100 within the scenario horizon (see §13d).
- **US and Eurozone data flow.** US consumer confidence and house-price data, plus any Eurozone CPI prints, feed the cross-Atlantic risk tone that the index tracks.
- **Middle East headlines.** Any escalation or de-escalation news on Iran and the Strait of Hormuz is a live, unscheduled catalyst for both oil and the broad index.
