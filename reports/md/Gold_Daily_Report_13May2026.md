# §12 Key Market Considerations
**Supply — neutral to mildly supportive**
Q1 2026 central bank purchases totalled an estimated 244 tonnes globally per the World Gold Council, with the National Bank of Poland (+31t), Uzbekistan (+25t), and the PBoC (+7t) leading the buyers list. PBoC reported holdings reached 2,313 tonnes, 9% of total reserves. Chinese mine production declined in Q1 on smelter-maintenance shutdowns. Net effect: structural sovereign demand remains the single largest supply-side absorption mechanism in the market and gives gold a hard floor in the $4,500–$4,600 zone. Status: PRICE-SUPPORTIVE (structural).
**Demand — mixed**
Investment demand: ETF flows positive year-to-date; physical premiums in wholesale markets remain firm at current levels per USAGOLD daily commentary. Jewellery / retail demand: stressed in India — PM Modi publicly called on citizens to pause gold purchases as the US-Iran war pressures the rupee and FX reserves. India is the world's second-largest buyer and spent ~$72 bn on gold imports in fiscal year 2026; a sustained Modi-driven slowdown would be a meaningful negative swing factor. Net: investment demand supportive, retail demand softening. Status: NEUTRAL with downside risk if Indian demand drag persists.
**Substitution and spread dynamics**
Gold-silver ratio compressed to 55.3 on 11 May after silver's +6.3% session, marking a multi-year low and signalling that money rotating into precious metals is currently favouring silver's industrial + monetary dual exposure (solar, AI data-centre buildouts, persistent silver supply deficit into a sixth consecutive year). For gold itself this is mildly negative on the rotation channel but supportive of the broader precious-metals complex thesis. Platinum was at $2,043 (slightly down on 11 May), palladium at $1,485 (slightly down). Status: NEUTRAL for gold standalone; supportive of complex.
**Policy and regulation — mildly gold-negative**
The Fed remains on hold at 3.50–3.75%. CME FedWatch shows only a 4.2% probability of a 25 bp cut at the June meeting; 95.8% expect no change. Market participants have moved to price in a ~25% chance of a rate hike by April 2027 after the hot April CPI. With borrowing costs sticky at current levels and the easing cycle effectively finished for the time being, the real-yield channel is gold-negative. PBoC and other emerging-market central banks continue to accumulate (structural support) — these two forces sit on opposite sides. Status: MIXED, slight net negative.
**Macro and market structure**
Dollar Index trading in a 98–100 range with a current rebound bias (12 May +0.35%). US 10-year auction results and the 13 May PPI are immediate macro pulses. The Iran-related Strait of Hormuz situation keeps Brent above $105 and oil-driven inflation passes-through into the Fed reaction function — paradoxically, geopolitical risk has been gold-negative on the rate-channel and gold-positive on the safe-haven channel, which is why price action has compressed rather than trended. COT positioning data not refreshed in this run; reference last available Reuters / WGC commentary. Status: NEUTRAL net effect.
**Near-term catalysts**
13 May — US PPI (April). 14 May — Initial jobless claims. 21 May — Manufacturing & Services PMI (May). Trump-Xi summit later this week, with Iran, Taiwan, AI, nuclear weapons, and critical minerals on the agenda. Any escalation in the Iran situation (Trump's 'massive life support' comment on 12 May) or any Hormuz reopening surprise will move the metal first. Status: PPI is the single largest near-term mover.
