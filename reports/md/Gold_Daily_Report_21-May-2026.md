# 12. Key Market Considerations [Updated]
## 12.1 Demand — Price-Supportive (structural)
Physical and official-sector demand remains the structural floor under gold. The World Gold Council reported Q1 2026 demand at a record level, with central-bank net purchases of 244 tonnes through the quarter, up roughly 3% year on year. This is a slow-moving, multi-quarter support that has cushioned the recent decline — Asian and London physical buyers were repeatedly cited defending the 4,500 area this week — but it has not been sufficient to offset the cyclical macro headwinds. Structural; price-supportive but currently subordinate to the rate channel.
## 12.2 Macro / Rates — Price-Negative (cyclical, dominant)
The dominant force on the current price is the hawkish repricing of US monetary policy. April CPI at 3.8% year-on-year — the highest since May 2023 — and a producer-price print described as the hottest in nearly four years have pushed CME FedWatch odds of a 2026 rate cut to near zero, with roughly a 40–50% probability of a December rate hike now priced. Higher-for-longer policy lifts the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding bullion. Cyclical but persistent; price-negative.
## 12.3 Macro / FX & Yields — Price-Negative (cyclical)
The US Dollar Index has climbed over 1% to around 99.4, near a six-week high, removing one of gold's most reliable support pillars. Simultaneously the US 10-year Treasury yield is near a one-year high (closing around 4.59% on 15 May) and the 30-year is near its highest since 2023. A rising dollar and rising real yields are a compound headwind transmitted directly into the gold price. Cyclical; price-negative.
## 12.4 Substitution / Spreads — Neutral
The gold–silver ratio tightened modestly from roughly 63.7 to 62.9 over the window, a marginal silver outperformance, while platinum tracked the broad metals de-rating. Real-yield comparison (the 10-year real yield proxy) is the more material substitution channel and currently argues against gold. Net: neutral, with a slight intra-complex tilt toward silver.
## 12.5 Policy / Geopolitics — Two-Sided (cyclical)
US–Iran tensions are the principal swing factor. The conflict has, on balance, been gold-negative through the inflation channel — surging oil prices have intensified inflation fears and reinforced hawkish rate expectations — even though a pure safe-haven episode would normally lift gold. Reported diplomatic overtures and a proposed temporary Iranian-oil sanctions waiver, if formalised, would reduce the geopolitical risk premium across commodities and could accelerate the correction; conversely, a fresh escalation is the clearest upside risk. Cyclical; two-sided.
