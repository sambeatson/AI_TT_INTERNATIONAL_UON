**12. Key Market Considerations**
**Supply**
Mine supply is broadly steady; the active swing factor is official-sector demand. Central-bank accumulation (PBoC, RBI and others) remains a structural support, with the World Gold Council reiterating that geopolitical factors should sustain reserve diversification through 2026. Price-supportive (structural).
**Demand**
Physical/retail demand is opportunistic: coin premiums are holding firm and dealers report dip-buying into the 4,400s, while Indian demand is subdued on import duties and price sensitivity. Investment demand (ETF / speculative) is the swing factor and is currently being reduced. Mixed, leaning neutral.
**Substitution & spreads**
The gold-silver ratio at ≈59.85 is near a multi-year low, historically a signal of silver outperformance in the next bull leg; for now both metals are falling together. Real-yield substitution (US 10Y real yield rising) is the key competing asset and is currently price-negative for gold. Price-negative (cyclical).
**Policy & macro**
The dominant macro force is the Fed's higher-for-longer posture: officials (Cook, Williams) have left room for hikes, with markets pricing roughly a 50% chance of a hike by December and core PCE risks flagged toward 3%. Elevated energy prices from the Hormuz disruption are feeding directly into CPI/PCE, giving the Fed cover to hold. Price-negative (structural-cyclical).
**Near-term catalysts**
The Thursday US PCE and second-estimate Q1 GDP cluster is the highest-impact scheduled item (see §13d). The binary swing factor is US-Iran: a confirmed ceasefire/MOU that reopens the Strait of Hormuz would ease oil-led inflation and could paradoxically support gold via lower yields, whereas continued strikes keep the dual oil-and-dollar headwind intact.
