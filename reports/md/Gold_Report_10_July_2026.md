**Sequence assessment**
The five-session sequence is: impulse up → failed extension → impulse down → deceleration → corrective bounce. This is a textbook trend-continuation sequence with the corrective leg currently in progress, not a bottoming sequence. The distinction rests on three observations. First, the 9 July high of 4,134.11 did not exceed the 8 July high of 4,103.50 by enough to break structure — it exceeded it, but the sequence of lower highs from 4,205.00 remains intact at the swing level. Second, the double-touch of 4,022.20 on consecutive sessions is a liquidity shelf, and shelves of that kind are more often swept than respected in a trending tape. Third, and decisively, RSI2 at 100.00 after a two-day bounce from an oversold extreme is the classic short-entry signature of the RSI2 methodology, not a long signal: in a confirmed downtrend, an RSI2 reading at the upper bound is a mean-reversion sell trigger.
Net movement across the window: −49.58 USD, or −1.19%. The tape has fallen despite closing the window with two consecutive up-days. That divergence between the recent candles and the window vector is the single most important short-term technical fact in this report.

# §12 Key Market Considerations
**Supply**
Mine supply is not the operative variable on a daily horizon and shows no disruption. Recycling supply typically rises with price and has been elevated through the 2026 highs; the drawdown from 5,595 has begun to reduce scrap flow at the margin, a mild structural support that operates over months, not days.
**Demand**
- Official sector — the decisive demand pillar. The PBoC reported its largest monthly gold reserve increase in more than two and a half years in June. The World Gold Council recorded a net 41 tonnes added to global central bank reserves in May, and estimated Q1 2026 net official additions of 244 tonnes. J.P. Morgan put Chinese net imports at 317 tonnes in Q1.
- Investment demand — deteriorating. HSBC lowered its 2026 average gold forecast to 4,560 USD/oz from 4,864, and cut its 2027 forecast to 4,925 from 5,000, citing changing perceptions of US monetary policy and dollar moves as the principal drivers of recent selling. HSBC also noted that central bank demand has slowed, while flagging a potential pickup in ETF buying later in the year.
- Jewellery and industrial — price-elastic and secondary. Gold's industrial share is small; the demand elasticity of jewellery buyers in India and China provides a slow-acting floor well beneath current levels.
**Policy**
The Federal Reserve held at 3.50%–3.75% at the 16–17 June meeting. The minutes, released 8 July, showed policymakers split: only a few favoured an immediate increase, but nine of eighteen expect the rate higher by end-2026, and officials expressed growing concern about inflation. LPL Financial's chief economist characterised the minutes as containing ambiguity, with future policy heavily contingent on Middle East developments. CME FedWatch now assigns roughly 83.4% probability to at least one hike this year, up from around 78% a week earlier. The probability that the Fed holds at 3.50%–3.75% in July stands at approximately 74.9%.
**Macro**
June nonfarm payrolls added 57,000 against a 110,000 consensus, the weakest in four months, with April and May revised down by a combined 74,000. Unemployment held at 4.2%; the participation rate fell to 61.5%, a five-year low. Initial jobless claims for the week ending 4 July fell 1,000 to 215,000, below expectations of 220,000–225,000; continuing claims held near 1.81 million. The labour market is softening on the payroll count but not on the claims count — an ambiguity that leaves the Fed room to prioritise inflation.
**Catalysts**
US Central Command confirmed strikes on Iran on consecutive days aimed at preserving Strait of Hormuz transit, through which roughly 20% of global energy supply passes. President Trump declared the interim memorandum of understanding with Iran to be over, threatened further strikes and a port blockade, and revoked the waiver permitting Iranian crude sales. Iran vowed large-scale retaliation against US bases. Oil surged more than 5% on 8 July, with two-session gains near 10%; WTI subsequently eased to around 73.10 on profit-taking. The 10-year Treasury yield reached a seven-week high of 4.597% before easing to 4.579%.
The critical mechanism to understand is that this geopolitical escalation is bearish for gold, not bullish. In a conventional risk-off shock, gold rallies. In this shock, the transmission runs through energy: Hormuz risk lifts oil, oil lifts inflation expectations, inflation expectations lift the expected policy rate, and a higher expected policy rate raises the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset. The haven bid exists but is being overwhelmed by the rates channel. Gold fell 0.9% on 8 July while crude surged over 5% — the split is explicit in the tape. A metals trading director at High Ridge Futures characterised the escalation as dragging down risk assets, gold included. Only a de-escalation that removes the inflation impulse, or an escalation severe enough to trigger a genuine flight to safety that overrides the rates channel, changes this.

**Aggregate**
Across all strategies and all triggered reconstructions, 11 of 15 possible card-sessions triggered. Mean R across all closed positions: +0.02R. TP1 hit rate across all triggered positions: 4 of 11 (36%). No position in the window reached TP2 or TP3 — every resolution occurred either at TP1, at the stop, or at the session close. That is a direct consequence of the 5-session window: the trades are one-session reconstructions and none was permitted to run.
What the record actually says is narrow but real: the pivot fade is the strategy the tape rewarded, the unconditional daily directional entry is the strategy the tape punished, and the live card structure — Trade 1 suppressed, Trade 2 produced — happens to align with that record. The alignment is coincidental. The suppression was mechanical, driven by the conviction threshold, not by the backtest.
**Limitations**
***Five-session windows are too small for statistical claims. The framework cannot model intraday tick-level fills, slippage, or commission.***
Two further limitations specific to this run are recorded rather than left implicit. First, the backtest reconstruction of Trade 1 uses a prior-session-extreme stop rather than the live EMA-anchored stop, because the EMA value was not corroborated for every historical session; the reconstructed Trade 1 is therefore a proxy for, not an exact replay of, the live card. Second, one session (9 July, Trade 1) resolved ambiguously — both stop and target lay inside the session range — and was assigned the adverse outcome by convention. A favourable-fill assumption would lift Trade 1's mean R from −0.03R to +0.37R and would invert the comparative ranking against Trade 2. That single assumption carries the entire comparative conclusion of §21d, and no reader should treat the ranking as robust.
