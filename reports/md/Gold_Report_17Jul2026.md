Rising crude on active Middle East escalation is, in the conventional reading, gold-supportive: geopolitical risk premium plus inflation hedging. The tape says otherwise. Gold fell 1.65% on the session on which crude rallied and Iranian targets were struck — the safe-haven channel is not firing. The geopolitical premium is being netted out and overwhelmed by the rates channel.
This contradiction is flagged, not resolved. It is the principal source of asymmetric risk in this report and is carried explicitly into §15 and §16. The mechanism reverses violently if escalation moves from strikes on targets to disruption of physical energy transit — the Bab el-Mandeb threat named by the Iranian IRGC is the specific trigger. At that point the safe-haven channel dominates the rates channel and every bearish structure in this report is invalidated on a gap, not a grind.

# §12 Key Market Considerations
**Supply**
- Mine supply is not a short-horizon price determinant for gold and shows no acute disruption. Above-ground stocks dominate the float; the marginal price is set by monetary demand, not production.
**Demand**
- Official-sector demand remains the structural support leg. Central banks have been net buyers for approximately fifteen consecutive years, and the World Gold Council's reserve survey implies continued accumulation as institutions diversify away from dollar exposure amid trade friction. PBoC and RBI activity remains the key watch.
- Investor demand is the weak leg. OCBC expects gold to decline through end-2026 on rising Treasury yields, a stronger dollar, and weaker investor appetite for precious metals — the investment channel is where the current selling originates.
- The critical asymmetry: central-bank demand is price-insensitive and slow; investor demand is price-sensitive and fast. Over a five-day horizon the fast leg sets the price. The official-sector bid establishes a floor over quarters, not sessions, and should not be counted on to arrest this move.
**Policy**
- CME pricing puts the probability of the Fed holding at 3.50–3.75% in July at 66.3%; the 29 July FOMC is the binding event. September hike odds sit near 51% — a genuine coin-flip and the live variable.
- Fed Chair Warsh has reaffirmed commitment to bringing inflation under control; his testimony reinforced expectations that rates stay restrictive, capping gold's upside on any inflation-hedge bid.
**Macro**
- Cooler-than-expected June CPI removed the near-term hike risk and is the one active bullish input — it eased dollar pressure earlier in the week and produced the 15 July bounce.
- That relief was overwhelmed within one session by the crude-driven revival of inflation expectations. The June PPI and Beige Book on 15 July did not reset the narrative in gold's favour.
**Catalysts**
- Middle East escalation — US strikes on Iranian targets on 15 July; Trump reports Tehran signalling willingness to negotiate. IRGC threats to expand targeting to regional energy transit, including Houthi action against Bab el-Mandeb shipping, are the tail risk.
- Both directions are live from the same event. De-escalation lowers crude, lowers inflation expectations, and paradoxically helps gold by removing hike pressure. Physical transit disruption spikes crude and gold together as the haven channel finally fires.
