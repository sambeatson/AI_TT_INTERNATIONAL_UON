Net status: neutral-to-supportive over the strategic horizon; not market-moving on the daily frame.

Investment demand has rotated rapidly with the rates/dollar regime — ETF outflows accelerated through last week per market commentary, while physical premia in Asia (Shanghai-London differential) held positive, indicating that allocated-bullion buyers in Asia continue to see sub-$4,600 as accumulation territory. India gold investment demand share has reportedly climbed to 40–45% (Deloitte). Net status: paper demand price-negative; physical demand price-supportive — the rotation between the two is the active battleground.
**Substitution and spread dynamics**
Silver fell ~12% week-on-week and is now trading near $76.75 — gold/silver ratio has expanded toward 59:1 from compressed levels, consistent with relative-strength flight to gold within the precious metals complex even as both fall. Platinum/palladium absorbed less of the move, indicating the sell-off is concentrated in monetary metals rather than industrial. Real-yield-equivalent (TIPS 10y at ~2.00% per FRED 14 May) acts as the principal substitute and is the dominant pressure channel.
**Policy and regulation**
Fed policy expectations have hardened decisively: CME FedWatch implied probability of a December 2026 rate hike has risen to ~48% from 14% a week earlier, with the probability of any 2026 cut effectively eliminated. Senate confirmation of a hawkish-leaning Fed Chair Kevin Warsh (per USAGOLD commentary) is part of the institutional re-rating. India introduced silver-import curbs over the weekend — read as an inflation-defensive policy that does not directly help gold but signals the broader EM policy stance. Status: price-negative.
**Macro and market structure**
US 10-year nominal yield at ~4.60% (highest since early 2025); 10-year real yield ~2.00%; USDX ~99.25. WTI crude has tested ~$103/bbl on the Strait of Hormuz closure narrative — the inflation-feed-through is now mechanical: higher energy → higher headline CPI/PPI → repriced Fed → higher yields → stronger dollar → lower gold. Status: price-negative on every link of the chain.
