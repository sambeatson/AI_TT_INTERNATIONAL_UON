# §5 Consensus Build Explanation
All eight observations are already denominated in USD per troy ounce on a spot basis, so no unit or delivery normalisation was required. The only genuine normalisation issue was timing. Gold fell through the European and US afternoon, so quotes captured at 16:00–17:30 UK sit systematically below those captured after 21:00 UK. Rather than blend across that drift, observations were weighted by proximity to the 22:00 UK capture point and by source tier.
Weighting followed the three-tier evidence hierarchy. Exchange-settled and price-reporting-agency data received full weight; aggregator feeds received partial weight; broker composites were admitted at one-third weight as third-tier corroboration only. The weighted median lands at 4,056.85, which is also the explicit daily close print carried by the highest-weighted source — a coincidence that materially strengthens the call.
**Exclusions.** One widely-syndicated aggregator page carried a 4,121.42 quote with a 4,130.09 prior close and a stated intraday range of 4,112.19 to 4,141.13. That range is internally inconsistent with the 4,036 session low reported by three independent sources and with the 105-point true range implied by the day's action; the page appears to have been captured before the afternoon decline and then cached. It was excluded from the consensus but its high and low were retained for the OHLC reconciliation in §6, where they corroborate the session extremes.
**Why this number is defensible.** Six of eight observations fall within a 13-dollar band around 4,056. The two outliers sit on opposite sides and are both third-tier. The dispersion of 0.44 percent is well inside the tolerance for a session in which the metal travelled more than 100 dollars, and the median is insensitive to removing any single observation.

**Sequence assessment.** Three higher closes followed by a single-session full retracement is a failed-breakout pattern, not a healthy pullback. The short-term state is transitional-bearish: the direction is down, but a 36.6 RSI2 is not oversold, so there is room to fall before mean-reversion mechanics engage.

# §12 Key Market Considerations
## Supply
- Mine supply is not the marginal price-setter on a daily horizon and showed no disruption in the window. Recycling flows typically rise after a sustained move above local-currency records; with gold roughly 28 percent below the January peak of 5,602, scrap supply is likely easing rather than building.
## Demand
- Central bank accumulation remains the structural floor under this market. Reported reserves show China adding 7.16 tonnes in the latest month to 2,313.46 and India adding 0.34 tonnes to 880.52. Russian reserves fell 21.77 tonnes, which is more plausibly reserve management than a demand signal. Net official-sector buying continues but at a pace that supports rather than drives price.
- Investment demand is the swing factor and is currently negative. The failure to attract haven flows during a two-day equity decline is the clearest available evidence that tactical money is positioned against bullion.
## Policy
- The Federal Reserve is in blackout ahead of the 28–29 July meeting. Consensus expects no change to the 3.50–3.75 percent target. The live question is September: market-implied odds of a hike have moved sharply within the week, quoted between 61 and 78 percent across sources on 23 July, and 68 percent earlier in the week. That repricing — a hiking cycle rather than a cutting one — is the single most important policy input for gold.
- The European Central Bank held its deposit rate at 2.25 percent on 23 July, noting that the full inflationary impact of the energy shock has yet to play out. Commentary through the session shifted toward a September ECB hike being more likely than not, reinforcing a globally synchronised hawkish tilt.
## Macro and catalysts
- US initial jobless claims for the week ending 18 July fell to 187,000 from 209,000 — described in one report as a 57-year low. A labour market this tight removes the primary argument for near-term easing and is unambiguously bearish for bullion.
- US CPI at 3.50 percent for June, down from 4.20 percent, shows disinflation in the official data — but the oil spike post-dates that print and threatens to reverse it. This is why the market is pricing hikes despite falling headline inflation.
- **Escalation is the two-sided catalyst.** A twelfth consecutive night of US strikes on Iran, a Houthi maritime embargo against Saudi Arabia with two tankers targeted, and a presidential threat of an attack "greater than anything before". So far each escalation has lifted oil, then yields, then the dollar — a net negative chain for gold. A move that directly threatened Hormuz transit would likely break that chain and produce a genuine haven bid.
