# 5. Consensus Build Explanation
Normalisation. All quotes were converted to the USD/oz, loco-London spot basis. The single futures observation (CME June contract, USD 4,655 settle on 22 May) was adjusted by removing the prevailing spot-futures basis to produce a spot-comparable figure of roughly USD 4,560; this carries an explicit basis-conversion assumption and is therefore used only as a directional cross-check, not as a core input.
Weighting. A weighted-median was applied, over-weighting the three clean 25 May spot-close reads (USA Gold, IFC Markets, FXEmpire) and the CNBC intraday quote, and down-weighting (i) the Bloomberg intraday peak, which reflects the session high rather than the close, and (ii) the futures-derived figure, which carries a conversion assumption. The four core spot reads span USD 4,556–4,571; their weighted-median falls at approximately USD 4,562.
Exclusions. No observation was fully excluded. Retail bullion-dealer premium prices were not gathered, consistent with the instruction to exclude retail spot. The cached vendor historical tables (Investing.com spot and futures) terminate on 14 May 2026 and therefore could not corroborate the 15–25 May sessions directly; those sessions are flagged in §19.
Why the number is defensible. USD 4,562 is the weighted-median of the cleanest available spot-close reads, is internally consistent with the basis-adjusted futures print, and sits squarely inside the independently observed 25 May session range. The Medium confidence rating reflects genuine intraday vendor dispersion on a thin holiday session rather than any conflict over the central tendency.

**Substitution / spreads (neutral).** The gold/silver ratio compressed to roughly 58.9 as silver outpaced gold on the rebound; platinum moved with the complex. Inter-metal spreads show a unified precious-metals bid rather than rotation, so substitution dynamics are neutral for gold specifically.
**FX (price-supportive; cyclical).** Dollar give-back on the de-escalation catalyst was a direct mechanical tailwind — see the USDX read in §10.
**Near-term catalysts.** The five-session horizon is dominated by the 28 May US Q1 GDP second estimate and weekly jobless claims (see §13d).
