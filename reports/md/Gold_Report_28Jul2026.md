# 12 · Key Market Considerations
## Supply — neutral (structural)
Mine supply is inelastic over the horizon; the swing factor is official-sector accumulation. Per the World Gold Council, central banks bought a net 244 tonnes in Q1 2026 and a further 41 tonnes in May, led by Poland, Uzbekistan and China. This is a structural, price-insensitive underpinning to dips.
## Demand — price-supportive (structural + cyclical)
Central-bank and safe-haven demand remain the dominant marginal buyers; ETF/investor demand is more price-sensitive and had thinned during the June correction but improved post-pullback. Asian physical demand is mixed — Indian discounts widened to a seven-week high on higher prices while Chinese buying improved.
## Substitution / Spreads — neutral
The gold–silver ratio compressed toward ~69.5–69.7 as silver (+0.79% on 27 Jul) outperformed, a mild signal of relative-value rotation into the white metal. Platinum firmed ~2%. Real-yield competition (US 10Y ~4.65%, near multi-month highs) is the key substitution headwind for non-yielding bullion.
## Policy / Rates — price-negative into the event (cyclical)
The 29 July FOMC is the fulcrum. Markets price ~30% odds of a hike this week and ~80% by September, with Fed funds at 3.50–3.75%. A hawkish hold or surprise hike lifts real yields and the dollar — negative for gold; a dovish tilt is the upside catalyst.
## Macro / FX — mixed
The weekend US–Iran de-escalation sent Brent/WTI down ~10%, easing the energy-inflation impulse that had pressured gold via the higher-for-longer channel — a supportive relief. A softer dollar on 27 July adds mild support. Offsetting this, risk-on equities (S&P 500, DAX) pull flows away from bullion.
