**Supply — neutral (structural)**
Mine supply is not the swing factor for daily gold pricing. Central-bank accumulation is the relevant supply-side dynamic: the People's Bank of China added eight tonnes to reserves in April, its largest single-month purchase in fifteen months, and World Gold Council data put Q1 net official-sector purchases at 244 tonnes, up 3% year-on-year. This is a structural, price-supportive backdrop that absorbs supply into weakness.
**Demand — price-supportive (structural)**
Physical demand strengthened into the selloff: Shanghai premiums held positive against London spot throughout the 19 May low, and dealer reports describe physical buyers stepping in beneath $4,500. Indian investment demand has reportedly risen toward 40–45% of the demand mix. The structural demand floor is intact even as financial-flow demand softens.
**Substitution & spreads — neutral**
The gold–silver ratio compressed toward 60:1 as silver outperformed on industrial and investment demand. Versus US real yields — gold's principal substitute as a portfolio hedge — the back-up in 10-year nominal yields toward multi-year highs raised the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset, a price-negative substitution signal partly offsetting the demand floor.
**Policy & regulation — price-negative (cyclical)**
April FOMC minutes confirmed that a majority of policymakers see a further rate increase as warranted if inflation stays persistently above target. Market-implied odds of a 2026 hike have firmed toward 40–55% depending on the source. A hawkish Fed lifts real yields and the dollar — the dominant price-negative channel for gold this month.
**Macro & FX — price-negative (cyclical)**
The dollar index reached a six-week high mid-week before easing toward 99 as US–Iran de-escalation headlines circulated. Elevated crude prices tied to Strait of Hormuz risk continue to feed inflation expectations, which is the mechanism keeping rate-hike odds alive. On balance the macro setting is price-negative for gold, with the dollar's late-week softening the main mitigant.
**Near-term catalysts — cyclical**
Within the five-day horizon the dominant scheduled item is the 22 May University of Michigan inflation-expectations release; US–Iran diplomacy and any further Treasury-yield moves are the unscheduled catalysts. All are carried into §13d and §15.
