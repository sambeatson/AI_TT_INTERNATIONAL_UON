**§1 Executive Snapshot**

The S&P 500 cash index closed Tuesday 19 May 2026 at 7,353.61,
completing a third consecutive losing session and a peak-to-trough
drawdown of 163 points (-2.17%) from the all-time intraday high of
7,517.12 set on 14 May. Market tone is risk-off but not stressed: VIX
rose to 18.61 (still below the 20 elevated-risk threshold), the Dollar
Index closed at 99.06 (modestly firmer week-on-week), and the DAX 40
closed up 0.38% at 24,479 --- a notable cross-asset divergence with US
equities. The three dominant drivers are (a) a backup in long-end
Treasury yields, with the 30-year briefly topping 5.19% and the 10-year
touching 4.687% on 19 May --- the highest 10-year reading since January
2025; (b) AI-infrastructure profit-taking ahead of NVIDIA\'s Wednesday
earnings, with semis leading the downside; and (c) geopolitical noise
around US-Iran tensions, partially offset by President Trump cancelling
planned strikes on Iran. The short-term technical posture is Bearish
continuation: closes have walked progressively lower over the last three
sessions, RSI2 is exhausted at 9.8, and the index closed at 2.3% of the
daily range on 19 May. The medium-term 25-session regime classifies as
Trending-bullish in transition: a strong uptrend from the late-April
7,046 low to the 14 May 7,517 high, now in its first material pullback.
Kaufman Efficiency Ratio remains above the trending threshold but is
rolling over. The single most important watch item for the 20 May
session is whether NVIDIA earnings (Wednesday after-close) provide the
catalyst either to retest 7,517 or to break the 7,349-7,385 demand zone
--- the latter would open a path to the 7,266 25-day average and
ultimately the 7,150 area where weekly and monthly pivot confluence
sits.
