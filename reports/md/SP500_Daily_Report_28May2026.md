# 12. Key Market Considerations

## Supply (equity float / issuance)

Net supply of large-cap US equity remains negative on a 12-month basis
--- corporate buyback activity has been the dominant net buyer of S&P
500 float. Q1 2026 earnings season corroborates: 84% of reporters beat
estimates, with EPS growth tracking 27.5% year-over-year.
Price-supportive.

## Demand (institutional positioning / flows)

Goldman Sachs (26 May) and UBS (prior week) raised year-end 2026 targets
to 8,000 and similar levels respectively. Both attribute the upgrade to
AI-infrastructure earnings power expected to deliver approximately half
of S&P 500 earnings growth in 2026. Polymarket S&P 500 close-of-2026
probability distribution places the modal bucket at 7,000--7,500, but
the right tail (above 7,500) has fattened materially in the last month.
Currently price-supportive --- though stretched.

## Substitution and spread dynamics

Comparison products (NASDAQ Composite, Russell 2000) are mixed: NASDAQ
at 26,656.18 (also at record); Russell 2000 at 2,920.54 has not made a
new high in 2026 --- small caps lagging is a typical late-cycle signal.
International equity (MSCI World ex-US) has also lagged the S&P 500 on a
year-to-date basis. Net: small-cap underperformance is a yellow flag for
breadth quality, but not an immediate sell signal.

## Policy and regulation

New Fed Chair Kevin Warsh was sworn in on Friday 22 May 2026, taking the
chair under a President publicly calling for lower rates. The Fed funds
policy rate is at 3.50--3.75% (steady at the May FOMC). Fed Governor
Christopher Waller commented on 22 May that he supports a rate increase
if inflation doesn't soon slow --- hawkish marginal. The market has
effectively priced no near-term cut. Tariff policy and prospective
US--Iran energy disruption are the two policy axes most likely to move
the price in coming sessions.

## Macro and market structure

WTI front-month at \~\$94 (Brent \~\$94) --- energy prices fell on 27
May intraday on US--Iran deal optimism. US 10-year yield (\^TNX): under
modest downward pressure on the diplomatic process but elevated by
Waller hawkish commentary. Gold (XAU) at \$4,464 fell 1.56% on 27 May as
risk-on flows reversed safe-haven demand. The composite read: easier
financial conditions on the energy and risk axis, partially offset by
hawkish Fed marginal commentary.

## Near-term catalysts

The dominant scheduled catalyst is the April 2026 Personal Consumption
Expenditures price index report released Thursday 29 May (US morning)
--- pre-market on the 28 May report-date session. A hot core PCE print
is the principal mechanism by which the rally could pause or invalidate;
a cool print would extend the trend. Secondary catalysts within the
scenario horizon: Q2 2026 GDP second estimate (also Thursday), personal
income and spending, weekly jobless claims.
