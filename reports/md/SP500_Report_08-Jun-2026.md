8\. Short-Term Technical Analysis

Session-by-session (see §6): Monday 1 June printed a clean bullish body
closing near its high with RSI2 pinned at 100 --- momentum fully
extended. Tuesday 2 June marked the record close (7,609.78), again with
RSI2 at 100 but a smaller real body and an upper wick, the first hint of
buyer fatigue at the highs. Wednesday 3 June was a decisive bearish
reversal: the index opened near the prior high and closed near the
session low, collapsing RSI2 to 14.9 --- the swing from extreme
overbought to oversold inside one session signals an inflection.
Thursday 4 June was an inside-ish recovery day (close 7,584.31, RSI2
35.3) that failed to reclaim the broken structure. Friday 5 June was a
wide-range bearish trend day: the index fell 2.64% to close at 7,383.74
with RSI2 at 13.2, the largest one-day decline of the year.

Sequence assessment: Exhaustion. Evidence --- two consecutive RSI2 '100'
prints at new highs followed by an outside bearish reversal and a
wide-range distribution day, with Friday's true range (\~224 points)
roughly 4.7× the 14-session ATR (\~47 points). This is the signature of
a momentum blow-off rolling into liquidation rather than orderly trend
continuation.

Nearest support / resistance from the validated 5-day structure: support
at the Friday close / indicative low zone 7,360--7,384; next resistance
at the Thursday close 7,584 and then the 7,605--7,621 supply shelf.
Judgement label: Exhaustion --- reversal risk.

*Consistency check: the short-term Exhaustion label is consistent with

9\. Medium-Term Regime & VOLator

Regime classification: Transitional (with residual bullish structure).
Supporting metrics --- directional persistence over the 25-session
window was strongly positive into early June (a multi-week grind to
record highs) but the last three sessions reversed roughly half of the
late-May advance; overlap between recent sessions has risen as the tape
became two-way. Median RSI2 across the block sits in the low-30s once
the two '100' readings and the two sub-15 readings are accounted for,
reflecting a regime losing its one-way character. Range-position bias
has flipped from upper-quartile to lower-quartile in a single week.

Bias: Neutral-to-Bearish as the directional component of the regime ---
the structure is not yet broken (the index remains well above its 50-
and 200-day averages, \~7,156 and \~6,858 respectively) but the
immediate momentum is down.

VOLator current readings (scaled −1 to +1): S&P 500 ≈ +0.9 and expanding
sharply (volatility well above its midpoint); VIX ≈ +0.95 and expanding
(risk-off confirmation); DAX ≈ +0.55 and expanding; USDX ≈ +0.3 and
rising modestly. Every series sits above its zero midpoint and is
expanding --- a broad, synchronized volatility-up regime, which for
equities is risk-negative.

Preferred trade protocol: in an exhaustion-into-transitional regime with
expanding volatility, the protocol is reduced-conviction, fade-strength
/ respect-oversold --- favour short setups on failed rallies into
resistance while being alert to a sharp mean-reversion bounce from
deeply oversold RSI2.

Kaufman confirmation: KER(13) ≈ 0.03, far below the 0.13 trend threshold
--- the medium window is classified ranging/inefficient. This agrees
with the Transitional regime label: the late-May trend has lost
efficiency. Where KER (ranging) and the residual bullish structure
disagree, KER takes precedence for regime labelling, yielding a
consolidated regime of TRANSITION.

\~6% above estimates, and AI-infrastructure capex continues to underpin

10-year yield above 4.5% and the 30-year above 5%, reviving fears the

for Friday's decline.

Sector / substitution (price-negative, cyclical): a semiconductor
de-rating led by Broadcom's disappointing guidance (AVGO −12.6%, MU
−7.7%, AMD −3.6%) drove the Nasdaq down 4.2% on Friday; because mega-cap
tech dominates index weight, this sector move did the heavy lifting in
the S&P's fall.

Policy / geopolitics (neutral-to-negative, cyclical): unresolved
US--Iran negotiations and Middle-East tension kept a geopolitical risk
premium in place and supported the dollar and oil; an Anthropic call for
a global pause in AI development added a sentiment overhang to the
AI-capex narrative.

Near-term catalysts (see §13d): the mid-June FOMC decision and
projections dominate; it is the single event most able to confirm or
reverse Friday's repricing.
