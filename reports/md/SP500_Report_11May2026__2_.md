## §12 Key Market Considerations

**Earnings (price-supportive).** The blended earnings growth rate for
the first quarter is 27.7% today, compared to an earnings growth rate of
27.1% last week and an earnings growth rate of 13.1% at the end of Q1.
Eighty-nine percent of S&P 500 companies have reported and 84% have
beaten EPS estimates --- the highest percentage of S&P 500 companies
reporting a positive EPS surprise since Q2 2021. Profit margins reached
a new record: The blended net profit margin for the S&P 500 in Q1 2026
stood at 13.4%. Source: FactSet Earnings Insight, May 8 2026.

**Valuation (price-negative tail risk).** The forward 12-month P/E ratio
is 21.0, which is above the 5-year average (19.9) and above the 10-year
average (18.9). Earnings strength justifies the multiple today, but any
Q2/Q3 deceleration will be less forgiving at 21× than at 18×. Source:
FactSet.

**Labour & macro (price-supportive near-term, mixed structurally).** For
the second straight month, U.S. jobs growth surpassed Wall Street's
expectations in April at 115,000, while unemployment stayed at 4.3%. The
April NFP beat reinforced the soft-landing thesis. However, with March
CPI at 3.3% year over year, accelerated from 2.4% in February, and with
the FOMC having held the federal funds rate at 3.50--3.75% with four
dissents (the most since October 1992), the inflation backdrop is
constraining.

**Iran ceasefire and oil (price-negative tail, currently dormant).**
U.S. and Iran's tenuous ceasefire agreement appeared to hold up through
the week. WTI crude finished the week near \$95.42/bbl and Brent near
\$100 --- well below the early-May intraweek peaks above \$106 WTI. A
breakdown in the ceasefire is the single largest binary downside risk,
mechanistically transmitting via oil → CPI → Fed hawkishness →
rate-sensitive sector P/E compression.

**Fed leadership transition (price-supportive on consensus, with tail
risk).** First, the Fed's transition to chair Kevin Warsh in June
introduces uncertainty about the path of rate cuts. Powell's term as
Chair ends 15 May 2026. The transition to a new Fed chair, Warsh, may
introduce a more dovish rate philosophy, influencing market expectations
and valuations. Markets are pricing dovishness; a hawkish pivot would be
a significant downside surprise.

**AI capex & megacap concentration (price-supportive, with concentration
risk).** Some 83% of S&P 500 companies have beaten profit estimates by
an average of 11% this quarter, but earnings growth is still led by the
magnificent seven megacap tech stocks, making up about a third of the
index and 55% of total expected Q1 earnings growth. The S & P 500 is
back at all-time highs, with earnings growth at the Magnificent Seven
companies still outpacing the other 493 stocks by more than 40%.
Concentration is the structural risk; an Nvidia earnings miss on 20 May
would not be a localized event.
