**§5 Consensus Build Explanation**

The consensus level was constructed under a weighted-median method that
down-weights stale or off-basis observations. Because the S&P 500 cash
index is a single exchange-published number at the close, the build is
straightforward: the official S&P Dow Jones value carries the highest
weight, the FRED published series provides the mandatory second
corroborating source, and the agreement is exact.

**Normalisation**

-   All six observations were checked against a single specification:
    the S&P 500 **cash index** level (not the ES futures contract, not
    an ETF proxy, not a total-return series), in USD index points, at
    the regular-session close.

-   No FX conversion is required --- the index is natively
    USD-denominated.

-   One observation (the Chicago-quoted \^SPX feed at 7,474.33) was
    **excluded** from the consensus because it is a different
    calculation basis; the 0.86-point gap is a basis artefact, not a
    data error, and is disclosed rather than averaged in.

**Weighting rationale**

-   Recency: all observations are from the 22 May close --- no recency
    penalty applies.

-   Source credibility: the index provider (S&P Dow Jones) and the
    official FRED series rank at the top of the evidence hierarchy and
    together fix the level.

-   Methodology transparency: aggregator and media feeds were used only
    as corroborators; they did not move the number.

The final figure of **7,473.47** is the most defensible consensus
because it is the official close, it is corroborated by an independent
official source at a zero delta, and it required no normalisation
assumptions. The reported precision is genuine --- not a derived
estimate.

**Earnings & corporate fundamentals --- price-supportive (structural)**

**Rates & monetary policy --- price-negative (cyclical, the dominant
risk)**

The 18--19 May sell-off was a bond-market event, not an
equity-fundamentals event. Long-dated Treasury yields surged --- the
30-year briefly topped 5.19%, its highest in nearly 19 years, and the
10-year reached a one-year high --- as markets pushed back the expected
timing of Federal Reserve rate cuts amid sticky inflation. A Bank of
America survey reported 62% of fund managers expect the 30-year yield to
reach 6%. Rising yields lift the discount rate applied to equity cash
flows and compress valuation multiples, the mega-cap technology cohort
most of all. Yields eased into the week\'s end, allowing the recovery,
but this remains the single most important swing factor. **Cyclical,
price-negative.**

**Geopolitics --- neutral to mildly supportive (cyclical)**

The US--Iran conflict and the status of the Strait of Hormuz dominated
the macro tape. Headlines oscillated between progress toward a deal and
renewed escalation; by 22 May, cautiously constructive signals were
supporting risk appetite while keeping oil --- and therefore inflation
expectations --- elevated. A genuine de-escalation that reopens the
Strait would be unambiguously supportive; a breakdown would re-ignite
the oil-and-inflation channel. **Cyclical, currently
neutral-to-supportive.**

**Macro & FX --- mildly price-negative (cyclical)**

The US dollar climbed to a six-week high (USDX \~99.3), a mild headwind
for multinational earnings translation (see §10). The University of
Michigan consumer-sentiment survey fell to a new low on gas-price and
inflation worries --- a soft-data warning that elevated energy costs are
bleeding into the broader economy. Oil remains well above pre-conflict
levels. **Cyclical, mildly price-negative.**

**Near-term catalysts --- see §13d**

The five-day horizon contains a heavy macro calendar. The **PCE
