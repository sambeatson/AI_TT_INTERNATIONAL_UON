**5. Consensus Build Explanation**

**Normalisation.** All observations are already expressed in S&P 500
index points, USD, on the regular cash-session close basis, so no FX,
unit or specification conversion was required. The single normalisation
decision was a basis filter: CME ES futures quotes (which trade at a
fair-value premium to cash) were excluded from the consensus build and
retained only as a directional cross-check, consistent with the rule
against blending futures and cash prices.

**Weighting.** A weighted-median method was applied. The three core
observations --- CNBC, TheStreet and Yahoo Finance --- each reported an
identical 7,519.12 close, so the weighted median is unambiguous at
7,519.12. The prior-session corroborators (FRED at 7,473.47,
Investing.com range) were down-weighted for recency as they describe the
22 May session, not the 26 May snapshot.

**Exclusions.** ES futures (basis mismatch). No retail CFD spread quotes
were used. No after-hours indications entered the cash OHLC.

**Why the number is most defensible.** Three independent feeds agree to
the hundredth of a point on the close, and the close is the field with
the strongest corroboration. The residual uncertainty sits in the
intraday High/Low fields --- which were not all confirmed against
exchange-grade settlement data this run --- and that uncertainty is the
reason confidence is held at Medium rather than High.
