# 12. Key Market Considerations
## Supply — price-negative (cyclical, with structural overhang)
The dominant supply story is the prospective reopening of the Strait of Hormuz. Both the US and Iran have signalled progress toward a ceasefire framework; ship-tracking data shows LNG tankers transiting Hormuz again, and the IEA's May Oil Market Report assumes flows gradually resume from June. Against this, the IEA still records record-pace global inventory draws and large supply losses from Gulf producers — a structural tightness that has not yet unwound. The market is pricing the expected normalisation ahead of the physical reality. EIA and IEA are the cited official sources.
## Demand — neutral to mildly price-negative (cyclical)
OPEC and the IEA have both trimmed 2026 global demand-growth forecasts, citing the conflict's drag on trade flows. US Memorial Day travel demand provided a brief seasonal support on 22 May. Net demand signals are secondary to the supply/geopolitical narrative this week.
## Substitution & Spreads — neutral
The Brent–WTI spread, which blew out to a record ~$35/bbl in April on Hormuz shipping dislocation, has compressed sharply as the disruption eases (Brent settled ~$103.54 on 22 May against WTI $96.60, a ~$7 spread). RBOB gasoline and heating-oil cracks remain firm on refined-product tightness from the Hormuz episode.
## Policy & Regulation — price-negative (cyclical)
US–Iran diplomacy is the policy lever. A signed ceasefire with Hormuz reopening would remove the bulk of the remaining risk premium; conversely a breakdown would re-introduce it rapidly. Strategic Petroleum Reserve actions remain a latent supply lever. The UAE's 1 May exit from OPEC adds uncertainty around spare-capacity accounting.
## Macro / Logistics / FX — mildly price-negative
A firmer dollar (USDX ~99) is a mild headwind for dollar-priced crude. Equity risk-on (S&P 500, DAX) reflects falling macro tail-risk rather than accelerating oil demand. Prompt WTI/Brent time-spreads ended the prior month near ~$5/bbl.
## Near-Term Catalysts — see §13d
The **EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report (Wed 27 May)** and any US–Iran ceasefire headline are the cyclical catalysts capable of moving price within the five-day horizon. Both are flagged in §13d.
