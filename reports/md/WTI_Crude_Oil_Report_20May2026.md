## §12 Key Market Considerations
### Supply — Price-Supportive (Severe)
The Strait of Hormuz has been effectively closed to shipping traffic since 28 February 2026 following military action. The EIA STEO assesses that Iraq, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, the UAE, Qatar, and Bahrain collectively had 10.5 mb/d of crude oil production shut-in during April 2026. The May STEO assumes the strait remains effectively closed until late May, with shipping beginning to pick up in June but not returning to pre-conflict levels until later in the year. The UAE departure from OPEC effective 1 May 2026 has materially reduced OPEC spare capacity — the EIA now expects OPEC spare capacity to average 2.5 mb/d in 2027 versus 3.8 mb/d previously. US Strategic Petroleum Reserve mechanics are under active review, with the administration described as looking for innovative ways to tap more crude from federal lands.
### Demand — Mixed (Net Mildly Bearish at the Margin)
US Memorial Day gasoline prices are at their highest in four years, which historically prompts some demand destruction at the retail level. Cross-asset reads in §10 (equities flagging) hint at growth-side caution. Against this, the IEA has warned that oil stockpiles are being drained at a record rate — suggesting that whatever demand destruction is occurring is more than offset by the supply shortfall. Net: demand is the weaker side of the balance but not in itself sufficient to break the bull case.
### Substitution and Spreads — Neutral / Mildly Supportive
Brent-WTI spread is wide ($3–6/bbl), reflecting the relative tightness in globally-tradable barrels (Brent) versus US-domestic-bound barrels (WTI). Heating oil prices have hit a six-week high, and June Nymex natural gas has reached an eight-week nearest-future high on US seasonal maintenance — corroborating broad energy-complex strength. Gasoline (RBOB) is under intra-day pressure from a stronger dollar but remains structurally elevated. The crack spread environment supports WTI.
### Policy and Regulation — Highly Price-Supportive (Conditional)
The 19 May Trump stand-down of planned strikes on Iran is the headline that drove WTI from $107 to $103. This is conditional support: the deal that was withheld can re-emerge. Iran has submitted an updated peace proposal that the White House reportedly views as insufficient. The US has issued a fresh waiver permitting the sale of Russian crude oil and petroleum products already loaded onto tankers, and seized an Iran-linked oil tanker in the Indian Ocean overnight. Sanctions architecture is in active flux. Russia and China are intensifying energy and geopolitical cooperation, per oilprice.com coverage. Any single headline can move price $3–5 in either direction.
### Macro and Market Structure — Mildly Price-Negative
Dollar strength (USDX) is a mild headwind for oil priced in USD; the cross-asset CONTRADICT signal (§10) reflects this. The Fed remains in a holding pattern and bond markets have not yet repriced for an oil-driven inflation shock at scale, but every additional week of $100+ crude raises the probability of a CPI surprise that resets risk-asset positioning. Speculative positioning data (CFTC COT) shows managed-money longs at elevated levels — the marginal long has been added at $105 not $80, which raises liquidation risk into a downside surprise.
### Near-Term Catalysts (cross-reference §13d)
The Wednesday EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report (15:30 UK summer time) is the single highest-impact scheduled event in the 20 May session window. The OPEC+ ministerial meeting cadence and any US Treasury sanctions update against Russian or Iranian energy entities are the unscheduled catalysts that have the most-direct mechanism to move the spot price.
