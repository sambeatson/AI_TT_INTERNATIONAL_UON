# 12. Key Market Considerations
## Supply — price-supportive (structural)
The Strait of Hormuz remains largely restricted, with the EIA assuming effective closure until late May and traffic only gradually recovering into June. The EIA estimates Gulf producers shut in around 10.5 million b/d of crude in April. US crude inventories have drawn for a fourth consecutive week and the Strategic Petroleum Reserve has been depleted by roughly 10 million barrels. This is a powerful, lasting supportive backdrop — it is the floor under the market.
## Demand — broadly neutral (cyclical)
There is no fresh demand catalyst in the window. The EIA frames large second-quarter inventory draws of around 8.5 million b/d, but these are supply-driven rather than demand-led. A firmer dollar and softer equities point to mildly cooler global demand expectations at the margin.
## Substitution & spreads — neutral
The Brent–WTI spread is wide — with Brent framed near USD 106 and WTI near USD 100, the gap of roughly USD 6–7 reflects the global vs US supply split, with the war premium concentrated in the waterborne Brent benchmark. Heating oil, RBOB gasoline and natural gas have moved with crude but offer no independent directional signal this week.
## Policy & geopolitics — the swing factor (cyclical)
The US–Iran diplomatic track is the dominant near-term driver and currently price-negative at the margin: optimism that an agreement could reopen Hormuz has pulled price off its highs, and satellite data showing supertankers crossing the strait reinforces that read. The countervailing risk is two-sided — President Trump has signalled the conflict could end “very quickly” but also warned strikes could resume if talks fail, and Iran has threatened to widen the conflict. The expiry of the waiver on Russian seaborne oil sanctions is a separate, mildly supportive supply-side policy development.
## Macro, logistics & FX — mildly price-negative (cyclical)
The Dollar Index is pressing a six-week high; a stronger dollar is a structural headwind for dollar-priced crude. Freight and war-risk insurance costs around the Gulf remain elevated, which supports landed prices but does not lift the futures benchmark directly.
## Near-term catalysts
- EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report — a fifth straight draw would be supportive; a build would accelerate the slide.
- US–Iran diplomatic headlines and any confirmed, sustained Hormuz reopening — the single largest swing factor.
- OPEC+ commentary and the evolving post-UAE-exit spare-capacity picture (the UAE left OPEC effective 1 May).
- US macro releases (PMIs, FOMC commentary) feeding the dollar and risk tone.
