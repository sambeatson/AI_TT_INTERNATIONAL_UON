**§12 Key Market Considerations**
**Supply**
**Middle East production shut-ins** currently in excess of 10 mb/d are propping prices via the geopolitical-risk premium. EIA STEO (12 May 2026) called Brent average of USD 117/bbl in April; supply remains structurally tight pending Hormuz status. **OPEC+** approved higher output quotas at the early-April meeting to plug the war-driven shortfall, but actual barrels remain constrained by physical disruption. **Saudi East-West Pipeline** reduced throughput by ~700 kb/d and Saudi capacity reduced ~600 kb/d. **Direction: NEGATIVE near-term** for prices on the unwind expectation; structurally still tight. Label: cyclical (war-driven, reversible inside 5-day horizon if a deal is signed).
**Demand**
EIA expects Q2 2026 inventory draws of ~8.5 mb/d on supply tightness. OPEC+ MOMR projects 2026 demand growth of ~1.4 mb/d. However, 2026 demand forecasts have been revised lower due to the price spike's elasticity drag — high prices are starting to bite gasoline demand in OECD. India is rapidly diversifying away from Middle East barrels (Investing.com 27-May). Direction: NEUTRAL — supportive on aggregate, but elasticity drag is showing. Label: structural for the elasticity story, cyclical for the inventory tightness.
**Substitution / Spreads**
Brent–WTI spread compressed to ~ USD 2.40 (Brent 92.71 vs WTI 90.31, Investing.com close) from peaks above USD 12 in March. Narrowing spread reflects US-side relief (Cushing access intact) versus continued global tightness — but the latter is dissipating fast on Iran-peace optimism. Crack spreads have eased as gasoline (RBOB) fell 6.1 % on 26-May. Direction: NEGATIVE for outright WTI on spread compression. Label: cyclical.
**Policy / Regulation**
**US–Iran indirect negotiations are the dominant policy variable.** Iranian state TV reported an “unofficial draft” of an interim deal on 27-May; the White House dismissed the report as fabrication, though Secretary of State Rubio confirmed talks may take “several days.” Unresolved sticking points: Iran's frozen assets, Hormuz transit guarantees, Iran's enriched uranium stockpile. SPR position remains a latent lever for the US administration. Direction: NEGATIVE for prices to the extent any deal-signal text is interpreted as Hormuz-reopening-imminent. Label: cyclical with structural ramifications.
**Macro / Logistics / FX**
USDX firm but range-bound near 99.16. US 10-year real yields stable. Two non-Iranian supertankers crossed Hormuz on 26-May for the first time in a week — first sign of physical normalisation, even before any formal deal. Speculative positioning: large-spec longs reportedly cut into the May spike top; CFTC report not yet refreshed for week ended 27-May. Direction: NEUTRAL-to-NEGATIVE. Label: cyclical.
