# 12. Key Market Considerations
## Supply
Price-supportive (structural). The Strait of Hormuz — through which nearly a fifth of global supply flowed pre-conflict — has been effectively closed since late February, with shut-in production and tanker disruption keeping a large risk premium in the price. The official monthly outlook estimates global inventories drawing sharply through 2Q and models a >$20/bbl premium for each month the strait's reopening is delayed. OPEC+ spare capacity is largely trapped behind the chokepoint. A brief Oman terminal explosion (since resolved) is a reminder of headline fragility.
## Demand
Mixed-to-negative (cyclical). Chinese crude imports fell to a ten-year low, reflecting softer refinery runs, and several analysts have cut 2026 demand-growth expectations. US refinery utilisation is firm (94.7%) but crude inputs slipped week-on-week.
## Inventories / balance
Price-supportive (cyclical, strong). The weekly status report (week to 29 May) showed commercial crude −8.0 mb to 433.7 mb (~3% below the five-year average); combined commercial + reserve stocks drew ~17 mb on the week and are down ~90 mb from their peak. Diesel narrowly held above the 100-mb threshold for the first time since 2003. This is a genuinely tight balance pulling against the demand and dollar headwinds.
## Macro / market structure
Price-negative (cyclical). A hotter-than-expected May payrolls print (+172k vs ~85k expected) drove the dollar to a ~1.75-month high and lifted Fed rate-hike odds (possibly by October), as higher energy costs feed inflation fears. A firm dollar and tighter-for-longer expectations are headwinds for dollar-priced crude.
## Near-term catalysts
The monthly energy outlook (9 Jun) and weekly inventory report (10 Jun) both fall inside the five-day horizon and are flagged in §13. Any concrete Strait-of-Hormuz reopening headline is the largest discretionary swing factor — the outlook assumes a reopening that, if delayed, adds materially to price.
