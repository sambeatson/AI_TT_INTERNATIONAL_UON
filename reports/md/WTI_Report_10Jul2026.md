# §12 Key Market Considerations
## Supply — bearish structurally, bullish acutely
The July Short-Term Energy Outlook, published 07 July, records that the United States and Iran signed a memorandum of understanding on 18 June to end the conflict and reopen the Strait of Hormuz. Following the signing and increased traffic through the strait, the agency raised its expectations for global oil production, and now expects most crude production to return to near pre-conflict averages by year-end, with the majority of shut-in output back online in the first quarter of 2027. Production shut-ins averaged 8.3 million barrels per day in June after peaking at 11.2 million in May.
Against that, the interim agreement collapsed on 07–08 July. US strikes on Iran resumed, Iran struck US bases in the region, and the President stated that in his view the ceasefire is over, warning of a possible blockade and of strikes on the Kharg Island export terminal. Vessel-tracking shows Hormuz transits declining, with visible activity concentrated on Iran-approved routes and limited movement on the US-backed Omani corridor. Roughly 20–21% of global crude and refined product trade transits Hormuz.
## Demand — bearish
The agency forecasts global oil consumption falling by an average of 1.2 million barrels per day across 2026, with 0.8 million of that decline coming from non-OECD countries, before rebounding by 2.0 million in 2027 once prices fall and supply normalises. High fuel prices and constrained availability have curbed consumption, particularly across Asia.
## Inventories — the pivotal ambiguity
US commercial crude fell over six consecutive weeks from roughly 449.8 million barrels in mid-May to about 408.4 million by the week ending 26 June — counter-seasonal, at a time of year when stocks normally build. The Strategic Petroleum Reserve is below its 2023 low, drawn down roughly 89.5 million barrels since the strait closed on 28 February as part of a 172-million-barrel coordinated release. Commercial stocks sit some 6% below the five-year average.
Then, on 08 July, the weekly report delivered a surprise build. The build must be read against a two-to-six-week lag between a Hormuz event and its appearance in US stockpile data: when tankers reverse course or reroute, previously en-route cargoes continue to arrive while new flows slow, which can produce a higher inventory print precisely when physical supply is tightening. A single-week build against a structurally deficient base is not a bearish signal of the same order as a build against a comfortable one.
## Policy and catalysts
- OPEC+ approved a further increase in production quotas of 188,000 barrels per day, maintaining the supply-restoration path despite Middle East risk.
- The agency's third-quarter Brent forecast was cut to $74/bbl, a reduction of $27/bbl from the prior month's outlook — one of the largest single-month revisions on record, and unambiguously bearish.
- Kharg Island handles the overwhelming majority of Iranian crude exports. An explicit presidential warning that it may be targeted is a tail-risk catalyst with no symmetric downside equivalent.
