# §5 Consensus Build Explanation
All quotes were normalized to the NYMEX front-month settlement basis, FOB Cushing. Two adjustments were required. First, the Barchart figure is published as a change from the prior settle rather than as a level; it was reconstructed as 74.45 − 1.32 = 73.13 at the time of capture, and reconciled forward to the settle. Second, the Trading Economics and live-tape figures are explicitly OTC/CFD-derived and carry a small persistent basis to the exchange print; they were retained as directional corroboration but down-weighted.
Weighting followed the weighted-median convention. The exchange-settled Investing.com close carries full weight as the only source publishing a validated daily OHLC series. Barchart carries full weight as an independent exchange redistributor. The two CFD-derived quotes carry half weight. Oilprice.com's intraday board was retained only as a mid-session cross-check because it is a level snapshot, not a settlement.
One exclusion was applied. The OilPriceAPI feed is excluded from the volatility pipeline entirely on a standing basis-mismatch rule; its Cushing storage figure was consulted for fundamentals only, and never touched the price consensus.
The final number of $73.40 is the most defensible because it is the only figure that is simultaneously (a) an exchange settlement rather than a snapshot, (b) internally consistent with the published high and low of the session, and (c) consistent with the independently reported −1.41% session change. The wide $73.16–$74.45 range is not noise — it is the honest span between the settlement and the post-settlement electronic tape on a day when price traversed $1.66 intraday.

The regime is: elevated volatility, decaying from a crisis peak, with a two-day secondary expansion attached to it. Elevated-but-decaying volatility inside a downtrend is a mildly bearish configuration; it says the market has already spent most of its energy on the way down and has not yet found a reason to spend an equivalent amount on the way up.
Kaufman confirmation. The 13-session efficiency ratio, EMA-3 smoothed, reads −0.17. Where they disagree, the Kaufman classification takes precedence for regime purposes, because it is measuring the ratio of net directional travel to total path length across a window long enough to contain both the impulse and the descent that preceded it. The short-term signal is measuring two candles. The five-session KER series — −0.40, −0.56, −0.37, −0.10, −0.07 — shows the ratio decaying toward the neutral band, which is the correct signature of a trend absorbing a countertrend shock. # §12 Key Market Considerations
## Supply
- The Strait of Hormuz is the operative variable. Vessel tracking shows a decline in transits, with most visible activity concentrated on Iran-approved routes while the US-backed Omani corridor saw limited movement. Substantial volumes continued to pass through the strait before the ceasefire collapsed, with some shipments appearing in tracking data only days later because of weak or disabled signals — meaning the observed decline overstates the actual disruption to date.
- The US has struck approximately 90 Iranian targets across two days, explicitly framed as degrading Iran's ability to threaten navigation. Critically, it has refrained from striking Iranian energy infrastructure. The President has, however, warned that future strikes may target the Kharg Island export terminal — which handles the overwhelming majority of Iranian crude exports — and has floated a naval blockade.
- The Strategic Petroleum Reserve stands at approximately 325.7 million barrels, having fallen a further 5.5 million barrels week-on-week and roughly 89.5 million barrels since the Hormuz closure of 28 February 2026 under an IEA-coordinated 172 million-barrel release. Total US crude including the SPR is near 734–743 million barrels, the lowest since October 1984. The shock absorber is thinner than at any point in four decades.
## Demand
- Refinery crude runs fell by 172,000 bpd in the week ending 03 July. This is the season in which runs should be climbing.
- Distillate inventories drew 4.98 million barrels to 103.6 million against a forecast 0.6 million-barrel build — a $5.6 million-barrel miss in the tight direction. Gasoline drew 1.904 million barrels to 212.1 million. Product demand is not the weak link.
- China's June CPI slowed to 1.0% year-on-year from 1.2%, a three-month low, missing a 1.1% consensus. Chinese demand-pull remains soft.
## Policy
- OPEC+ has been increasing output into a market where the fear has shifted from shortage to glut. That posture predates the current escalation and has not been publicly revised.
- A blockade of Iranian exports, if executed, is a supply-side policy action of first-order magnitude. It is currently rhetoric.
## Macro
- The US 10-year yield climbed to 4.57% as rising oil reignited inflation concerns, before easing to 4.563%. The rates market is treating this oil move as an inflation impulse, not a growth impulse.
- At least one commentator on 09 July raised the possibility that equities are not pricing a further Federal Reserve rate hike in the second half of 2026, describing the environment as an ongoing inflation problem and explicitly noting it is not just the oil prices.
## Catalysts
- EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report, Wednesday 15 July, 15:30 UK. The week ending 03 July printed a 2.998 million-barrel commercial crude BUILD to 411.3 million barrels — the first rise after ten consecutive weekly declines, against an expected 2.4 million-barrel draw. Cushing was near-flat at −52,000 barrels, holding around 19.7 million barrels, roughly 26% of the hub's ~76 million-barrel working capacity and some 12.1 million barrels below its five-year average.
- Any US strike on Kharg Island or a formal blockade announcement. Unscheduled, binary, and directionally unambiguous.

# §14 Macro Context
## Rates
The US 10-year yield reached 4.57% on 08 July, explicitly attributed to jumping oil prices reigniting inflation fears, and settled at 4.563% on 09 July, down 0.004. The 30-year sits at 5.069%, the 2-year at approximately 4.23% implied by a 10s2s spread of 31.32 basis points that widened 15.27% on the session. A steepening curve alongside a falling front end is the shape of a market pricing inflation risk into the long end rather than pricing growth. For crude, this is a warning: the rates market believes higher oil prices will be met with tighter policy, and tighter policy is demand-destructive.
## Inflation
China's June CPI at 1.0% year-on-year, a three-month low, missing consensus. Disinflation in the world's largest crude importer. Against this, the observation from the equity tape that the current environment represents an ongoing inflation problem for the remainder of the year, and that it is not just the oil prices. The two are not contradictory — they describe different economies. They are contradictory for oil, because Chinese disinflation caps demand while US inflation caps policy accommodation.
## Liquidity and FX
The Dollar Index reads 100.63–100.95, having opened at 101.06 and traded a $0.28 range. It backed off the week's highs as the US and Iran exchanged strikes. This is the §10 counter-1 reading: falling. Mechanism for WTI: a softer dollar reduces the cost of crude to non-US buyers and marginally loosens global financial conditions, which is supportive. But the magnitude is trivial and the cause is safe-haven rotation, not an easing cycle. The dollar is not currently a driver of crude; it is a passenger.
## Energy cross-reads
The Brent–WTI spread stands at approximately $4.32 with Brent futures near $77.72 against WTI at $73.40. RBOB gasoline fell 1.07% on 09 July after rising 5.06% on 08 July, tracking crude almost exactly and offering no independent signal. Natural gas rose 0.65% to $3.239. The Brent–WTI spread near $4.30 is wide enough to keep US Gulf Coast export arbitrage open, which drains Cushing and is structurally supportive of WTI relative to Brent — consistent with Cushing at 19.7 million barrels, roughly 12.1 million barrels below its five-year average.

VIX at 15.84–16.90 depending on the capture point, having fallen 6.27% on the session after rising 4.77% on the prior day. This is not a stressed equity volatility regime. Historically, one of those two markets is wrong. Given that the disruption is a physical chokepoint rather than a financial-system event, the equity market's calm is the more defensible of the two positions — which is, quietly, a bearish datum for crude.
