## §12 Key Market Considerations
**Supply — strongly price-supportive**
- EIA STEO (12 May): 10.5 mb/d of crude oil shut-in across Iraq, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, UAE, Qatar, and Bahrain in April. The May STEO assumes Strait of Hormuz traffic remains effectively closed until late May with a slow June recovery; pre-conflict throughput is not expected until later in 2026 (Source: EIA STEO May 2026 release).
- Saudi Aramco CEO Amin Nasser (cited by Trading Economics, 11–12 May): ~100 million barrels of weekly supply being lost; normalisation may not occur until 2027 if disruptions persist.
- OPEC structural change: UAE departed OPEC effective 1 May 2026. EIA now expects OPEC spare capacity to average 2.5 mb/d in 2027 vs 3.8 mb/d previously — a structural reduction in the supply buffer.
- US inventories: EIA week-ending 1 May showed crude stocks down 2.3 mb on refinery throughput at 16 mb/d — consistent with persistent tightness.
**Demand — neutral-to-modestly-supportive**
- Refinery inputs at 16 mb/d are seasonally strong; refiners are running into the summer driving season.
- Global demand has not yet shown clear destruction signs at current price; EIA STEO maintains 2Q26 inventory draw of 8.5 mb/d which is supply-driven, not demand-driven.
- Asian demand is partly disrupted by the same Hormuz issue (less crude reaching Asian refiners), but propane substitution from US exports is partly offsetting (per EIA STEO).
**Substitution & spread dynamics**
- Brent–WTI spread ~$4–5 (WTI at $101.87 vs Brent at ~$103–106) — tighter than in oversupplied regimes; consistent with the Gulf supply shock being more impactful on the global benchmark than the US benchmark.
- Heating Oil and RBOB Gasoline crack spreads (not separately fetched here) typically widen in war-premium regimes — supportive for product-driven crude demand at the refiner.
- Natural Gas (NG=F) is not directly tied to the war premium in the same way; the NG-WTI energy-complex divergence is a feature, not a bug.
**Policy & regulation — price-supportive on the geopolitical leg, neutral on US production lever**
- Trump Administration is publicly considering escalation: 'meet with national security team to consider renewed military action' and 'escort commercial vessels through the strait' (Trading Economics, 12 May).
- US Strategic Petroleum Reserve releases / refills not currently announced as a near-term lever; the SPR is reported to be a topic the administration is 'looking for innovative ways' to use (OilPrice 6 May coverage of the federal-lands initiative).
**Macro / market structure**
- USDX firmer (98.05): partial headwind to oil through the inflation/financial-conditions channel.
- Inventory curve: backwardation likely steep given physical supply shortage — front-month firms hardest.
- Speculative positioning (COT): not separately fetched; the typical pattern in war-premium regimes is long-WTI managed-money increases week-over-week.
**Near-term catalysts**
- EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report — Wed 14 May (week ending 8 May). Highest-impact watch item for the next 5 sessions.
- US CPI release for April — 13 May. Sets the rate-path tone that drives USDX (the WTI counter).
- OPEC MOMR — typically mid-month; any commentary on Gulf shut-ins is highly price-relevant.
- Trump–Xi summit (this week) — agenda includes Iran; surprise de-escalation is the principal downside risk.
