# §5 Consensus Build Explanation
Normalisation. All observations were reduced to a single basis — NYMEX front-month settlement, USD/bbl, Cushing delivery. Two source classes required treatment. CFD-derived quotes (Trading Economics at 79.06) track the benchmark but are not exchange-settled; they are retained as corroborative only, with basis explicitly declared. Spot Cushing (the official EIA series) is a physical assessment and is not spliced into a futures series.
Weighting. The weighted-median method was applied, which down-weights stale aggregator data while preserving exchange and official reads. The exchange settlement cluster (79.60–79.75) carries the weight; the 79.06 CFD print and the ~80 narrative reference sit outside that cluster and do not move the median.
Exclusions. One retrieval attempt returned the TOCOM yen-denominated contract (JCOc1, ¥72,710) rather than the NYMEX USD contract. This is a basis mismatch of exactly the type that caused a prior volatility-engine failure, and it was discarded entirely rather than converted — currency conversion of a different exchange's contract would fabricate a price that never traded. A dedicated oil-price API is excluded from the volatility pipeline under standing instruction.
Why $79.75 is most defensible. It is the exchange settlement of the contract named in the price basis, printed by the primary source and corroborated within $0.15 by an independent newswire settle. The $0.15 gap between 79.60 and 79.75 reflects contract-month and cut-off timing, not a data error; taking the settlement print of the stated basis avoids importing that ambiguity.

# §12 Key Market Considerations
## Supply — Price-supportive near-term, price-negative structurally
- Cyclical: The US reinstated a naval blockade of Iranian ports near the Strait of Hormuz and conducted a seven-hour strike operation against dozens of military assets, per official statements reported 15 July. Roughly 27% of the world's seaborne crude transits the Strait.
- Structural: Official supply forecasts were raised materially following the 18 June memorandum of understanding, with most shut-in production expected back near pre-conflict levels by year end and the majority restored by Q1 2027. Production shut-ins averaged 8.3 million b/d in June after peaking at 11.2 million b/d in May.
- Cyclical: US crude inventories fell by 1.7 million barrels last week — a modest draw, mildly supportive.
## Demand — Price-negative
- Structural: Global oil consumption is forecast to decline by an average of 1.2 million b/d in 2026, with 0.8 million b/d of that from non-OECD countries — high prices, fuel shortages, and government curtailment have durably reduced consumption.
## Substitution / Spreads — Neutral to price-supportive
- Cyclical: Brent settled at $84.95 against WTI at $79.60, a spread near $5.35. Brent's premium reflects the fact that the disruption is concentrated in seaborne Gulf flows rather than at Cushing — the risk premium is being expressed more in the global marker than the US benchmark.
## Policy / Regulation — Price-supportive, high variance
- Cyclical: The administration abandoned a proposed 20% Hormuz transit fee. Further strikes on Iranian power plants and bridges were threatened publicly within the week.
## Macro / Logistics / FX — Mildly price-supportive
- Cyclical: June headline CPI slowed to 3.5% from 4.2%, with monthly prices falling 0.4% — the first monthly decline since 2020. This softened the dollar (see §10), a marginal support for crude.
## Near-term Catalysts
- EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report (Wednesday) — the highest-frequency supply/demand input within the horizon.
- Any further US strike package or Iranian retaliation against shipping — the dominant variance driver.
- Next official Short-Term Energy Outlook (11 August) — outside the 5-day horizon but frames the structural baseline.
