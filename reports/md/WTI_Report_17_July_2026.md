# §8 Short-Term Technical Analysis
The five-session sequence (see §6) is one of the cleanest reversal-to-impulse structures the instrument has printed this quarter, and it is worth walking candle by candle because the character changes twice.
9 July opened at 74.95, failed within eighteen cents of the open, and closed at 72.08 — a bearish session that gave back nearly all of the prior day's 4.4% advance, with the close in the lower third of the range. 10 July continued the slide but with visibly less conviction: a 2.39-point range against an ATR of 2.97, a close at 71.41 only marginally below the prior close, and a low at 70.77 that held above the early-July base. This is exhaustion, not distribution — sellers were still in control but had stopped being paid for it. RSI2 at 41.7 confirms the momentum had already left the downside.
13 July is the pivot of the entire sequence. Open 73.69, high 78.58, low 72.61, close 78.14 — a 9.42% advance closing within forty-four cents of the session high, on a range of 5.97 points, roughly 1.8× the prevailing ATR. A close that near the high of an outsized range is a control signal: the buyers who initiated it were not taking profits into the close. 14 July extended, printing the swing high at 81.27 and closing at 79.85, though the close fell 1.42 points off the high — the first sign of supply appearing near the round number. 15 July then compressed hard: range of 1.08 points, barely a third of ATR, close at 79.75 against an open of 79.64, essentially unchanged.
The sequence assessment is therefore: reversal (9–10 Jul) → impulse (13–14 Jul) → consolidation (15 Jul). The 15 July inside-range session is the diagnostically important one. A narrow-range session that holds nearly all of a two-day 8.34-point advance is a continuation pattern far more often than a topping one — the alternative reading, distribution, would require the range to be wide and the close to be weak, and it was neither.
The caveat is unambiguous and it is RSI2. Three consecutive readings above 90 (91.1, 93.8, 90.6) put the tape in the top decile of its two-period momentum distribution. On a mean-reversion reading this is a sell signal. On a trend-continuation reading — which is what the Kaufman classification in §9 supports — persistent RSI2 above 90 is a hallmark of a strong trend, not a warning. The two readings are not reconcilable and this report does not pretend otherwise; the direction score in §21a resolves it by weight, not by argument, and the trade construction in §21b is built to survive being wrong about it.
Key short-term levels: swing high 81.27 (14 Jul), swing low 70.77 (10 Jul), and the 4-session fib swing derived from that range gives 78.79 (23.6%), 77.26 (38.2%), 76.02 (50%), and 74.78 (61.8%).
# §9 Medium-Term Regime & Volatility
Over the 25-session regime window, WTI has moved from 79.44 (15 June) to 79.75 (15 July) — a net change of +$0.31, or 0.4%. That flatness conceals a violent path: a collapse to 67.04 in early July, a 17.5% base-to-peak recovery, and a return to the starting point. The regime classification is TRANSITION rather than TREND_UP. A trend requires directional net progress over the regime window and there is none; what exists is a completed V-shaped round trip. This is the single most important structural fact in the report, and it is the reason the medium-term contribution to the direction score is small.
Volatility read: ATR(14) sits at 3.12, having peaked at 3.28 on 14 July. Against the 25-session distribution this is elevated but not extreme — the true range expanded sharply through the 13 July impulse and has been contracting since, with the 15 July range of 1.08 points falling to roughly a third of ATR. The volatility regime is best described as expanded-and-cooling: high absolute volatility, decaying at the margin. For position sizing this matters more than the direction — a 3.12 ATR on an $80 barrel is a 3.9% daily true range, and stops placed inside one ATR will be taken out by noise alone.
Kaufman confirmation: the efficiency ratio (13-session, 3-period EMA smoothed) reads +0.38 signed, with a raw value of 0.53. Both are far above the 0.13 trending threshold, so the classification is TRENDING UP.
**Conflict — regime vs Kaufman.** The 25-session regime says TRANSITION; the efficiency ratio says TRENDING UP. This is not resolved silently. The Kaufman reading takes precedence for execution purposes over the next five sessions, and here is why: the efficiency ratio measures directional efficiency over a 13-session window, which straddles the July base and the current impulse, and it is telling us that recent price movement is converting into net directional progress with unusually little wasted motion. The 25-session regime label is measuring a window that begins before the collapse, so it is arithmetically anchored to a starting point the market has already round-tripped away from. For a five-day horizon the 13-session read is the more informative instrument. For anything longer, the TRANSITION label is the honest one and the trend read should be discarded.

# §12 Key Market Considerations
**Supply.** The dominant supply story is the reversal of the June de-escalation. The 18 June memorandum of understanding between the US and Iran opened the Strait of Hormuz and drove the entire 2Q collapse — the July Short-Term Energy Outlook explicitly attributes the $22/bbl month-on-month fall in June Brent to expectations of returning supply. That premise is now partly broken. US strikes on Iranian missile storage and launch sites near the strait resumed on 15 July, a naval blockade of Iranian ports has been reinstated, and there are reports the administration is weighing seizure of Kharg Island — Iran's principal export terminal, and a target whose loss would remove roughly Iran's entire seaborne export capability from the market. Separately, Ukrainian strikes on Russian refining and tanker assets continue to remove barrels at the margin. Against this, Gulf exports rose 6.5 mb/d in June to 16.1 mb/d, still 8 mb/d below the pre-war average, and the official expectation is that most shut-in production returns by 1Q27.
**Demand.** Refined product cracks and margins hit four-year highs in early July as crude fell faster than products, which is a genuine demand signal for refiners even if it flatters the headline. Global refinery runs rose 1.5 mb/d in June but remain 6 mb/d down year-on-year, with Middle East export refineries still offline and Russian throughput curtailed. The demand picture is therefore constrained by refining capacity rather than by consumption.
**Inventories.** US commercial crude drew 1.7 million barrels in the reported week — bullish against a consensus expecting a build, and the proximate cause of the firm 15 July tape. This is a weekly datapoint against a structural counter-current: global observed inventories rose in June for the first time in four months, with oil on water swelling 117 mb, outpacing a 96 mb onshore draw. Barrels are moving from tanks onto ships, not disappearing. The Cushing hub reading specifically was not independently corroborated for this session and is not relied upon.
**Policy.** Trump abandoned the proposed 20% Hormuz transit fee on 14 July, arguing forgone revenue would be offset by Gulf investment — a de-escalatory gesture that removed a direct cost from the freight chain and briefly capped the rally, before being overtaken by the strike escalation the following day. The policy signal is genuinely two-directional and changing on a 24-hour cycle.
**Catalysts.** The Brent–WTI spread sits near $5.60 (85.36 vs 79.71), a normal transatlantic differential that signals no acute US-specific dislocation — the move is global, not a Cushing story. The forward curve, per the July Short-Term Energy Outlook, prices Brent at $74/bbl for 3Q26 and $65/bbl for 2027, materially below the current strip: the official view is that today's price is a risk premium that decays.
