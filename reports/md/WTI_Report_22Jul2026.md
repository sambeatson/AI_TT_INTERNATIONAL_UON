# §12 Key Market Considerations
## Supply — price-supportive (dominant)
The mid-June US–Iran MOU that had reopened the Strait of Hormuz has broken down; renewed strikes and a fresh maritime embargo threat against Saudi traffic in the Red Sea have re-imposed a supply-risk premium. A separate outage at the Caspian Pipeline Consortium Black Sea terminal is curtailing Kazakh exports. The IEA notes Middle East export refineries have yet to restart and global observed inventories, while up 21 mb in June, remain historically thin with the US SPR at its lowest since April 1983. EIA STEO still models global stock draws of 2.2 mb/d in 3Q26.
## Demand — neutral to soft
EIA projects 2026 global consumption down ~1.2 mb/d, with most demand destruction in import-reliant Asia. This caps the structural upside and underpins the bank surplus thesis for H2. Product markets are the exception — gasoline and diesel cracks at four-year highs are the tight spot.
## Substitution & spread — supportive
Brent traded ~$91 vs WTI ~$84 on 21 Jul, a wide ~$7 Brent–WTI spread signalling global (waterborne) tightness relative to the US hub. Multi-year-high crack spreads pull crude into refineries.
## Policy & macro — mixed
Markets now price ~55% odds of a September Fed hike as the oil shock lifts headline-inflation risk; the Fed is in blackout ahead of next week's FOMC. A firmer-rate path is a medium-term headwind for oil demand even as the near-term risk bid dominates. SPR is a depleted lever, limiting the US administration's ability to cap prices.
## Near-term catalysts
Watch (see §13d): Wednesday EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report; any concrete US–Iran ceasefire text (the 10-day truce proposal); Hormuz/Red Sea tanker headlines; next-week FOMC. A credible ceasefire is the primary downside catalyst; a confirmed Hormuz closure is the primary upside catalyst.
