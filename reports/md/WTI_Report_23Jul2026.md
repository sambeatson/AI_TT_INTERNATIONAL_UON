# §5 Consensus Build Explanation
The observation set is unusually clean on basis but unusually dirty on timestamp, and the build reflects that asymmetry. Four of the six observations are already on front-month NYMEX futures basis and required no normalisation. The Brent observation was converted using the prevailing Brent–WTI differential of approximately $7.3/bbl, which is itself wide relative to the 2026 average and reflects the fact that the current risk premium is being priced disproportionately into the waterborne global benchmark rather than the landlocked US hub — a point developed in §12.
Weighting followed the standing evidence hierarchy: exchange-settled data ranks above price reporting agencies, which rank above aggregators. The CME settlement therefore carries the dominant weight. The weighted median rather than the mean was used specifically to prevent the mid-session Oilprice observation at $86.42 and the stale Investing.com print from dragging the central estimate below the settled level; a simple mean of the five price observations would have produced roughly $85.5, which would misrepresent where the contract actually cleared.
One observation was reclassified rather than excluded. The Investing.com quote at $83.17 is timestamped to the 21 July session rather than 22 July; instead of discarding it, it has been retained as the prior-session corroboration point, where it independently confirms the 21 July close of $83.17 used in the OHLC table at §6. No observation was excluded outright.
The final number is the most defensible available because it is anchored to the only observation in the set that represents an actual clearing price with settlement authority behind it, and every other observation in the set is within 50 cents of it once basis-adjusted. The genuine uncertainty in this report is not measurement uncertainty — it is that the measured price is contingent on a military situation with a sub-daily headline cadence, which is a §16 and §19 problem rather than a §5 problem.

The session opened at $82.38 — a gap down of $1.67 from the prior close — ranged only $1.75, and settled at $82.45, essentially unchanged from its own open. Two features are notable. First, the session's low of $81.85 held above the 17 July low of $81.10, preserving the higher-low structure. Second, the range contraction to half the prior session's is a consolidation signature rather than a reversal signature; a genuine reversal from a two-day thrust would ordinarily produce range expansion against the trend, not compression. RSI2 reset from 98.3 to 55.4, discharging the overbought condition without any structural damage — which is precisely the condition that permits a trend to continue rather than terminate.

Reuters reporting on the session noted prices weighed by mediation reports, which is consistent with the intraday dip and the subsequent recovery when mediation prospects faded. 22 July is the session that requires the most careful reading. It opened at $83.30, traded to $88.60 — the highest print in six weeks and a $5.30 excursion from the open — then settled at $86.85, surrendering $1.75 of the advance. The $5.55 range is the widest of the block at 1.71× average true range. The bar is unambiguously bullish in net terms: +$3.68 on the close, a decisive higher high, and a higher low. But the close in the middle third of the range, following a five-session advance, on the widest range of the block, is the classic signature of supply entering at the highs. # §12 Key Market Considerations
### Supply — PRICE-SUPPORTIVE (cyclical, with structural undertone)
The supply picture is the dominant driver and it is operating through disruption risk rather than realised disruption. US strikes on Iranian targets have continued for an eleventh consecutive day, with the stated operational aim of degrading Tehran's capacity to threaten shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. The Strait remains open. That distinction is the single most important fact in this report: the market is pricing a probability-weighted disruption, not an actual one, which means the premium is by construction reversible.
Secondary supply channels have compounded the effect. Houthi activity against Red Sea shipping is forcing tanker rerouting, and renewed attacks on the Caspian Pipeline Consortium terminal on Russia's Black Sea coast threaten a key export route for Kazakh crude — extending the disruption geography beyond the Gulf. Reporting indicates strikes between the US and Iran have halted oil and fuel exports from the GCC, which if sustained would move the situation from priced-risk to realised-loss.
Against this, US commercial crude inventories built 2.0 million barrels in the week ending 17 July to 411.7 million, against an expectation of a draw — a bearish surprise on the week. The American Petroleum Institute had estimated a 2.603 million barrel build a day earlier, so the direction was signalled. But the level context inverts the signal: at 411.7 million barrels, stocks remain 6% below the five-year average, and commercial inventories have shed just over 57 million barrels across the preceding thirteen weeks. Cushing hub inventory — the delivery point for this contract — fell a further 737,000 barrels over the reporting period, which is the more relevant number for front-month WTI specifically. The Strategic Petroleum Reserve released another 5.1 million barrels to 316.5 million, the lowest level in over 43 years and roughly 420 million barrels below capacity. The structural point is that the buffer available to absorb a genuine Hormuz disruption is thinner than at any point in four decades, and that is a durable condition rather than a weekly data point.
### Demand — NEUTRAL (cyclical)
Refinery utilisation ran at 96.1% of operable capacity, processing 17.1 million barrels per day, down 58,000 b/d week-on-week — a marginal deceleration from a very high base. Gasoline production increased to 9.7 million b/d and distillate production to 5.3 million b/d. Crude imports averaged 5.8 million b/d, up 117,000 b/d. Product inventories built across the board on the EIA read: gasoline +0.8 million barrels (7% below the five-year average), distillate +1.4 million (10% below average). The demand signal is neither confirming nor contradicting the price move — utilisation near 96% is a seasonal-peak reading that leaves little upside, and the product builds against below-average levels are ambiguous.
### Substitution and spreads — PRICE-SUPPORTIVE (cyclical)
Brent settled around $94.13, up 3.43%, against WTI at $86.85 — a spread of approximately $7.28. This is wide relative to the 2026 norm and it is analytically informative: the waterborne global benchmark is absorbing more of the risk premium than the landlocked US hub, which is the expected configuration when the disruption risk is concentrated in a maritime chokepoint. Brent traded to a six-week high of $94.40. The widening spread signals global tightness rather than US oversupply, and it supports WTI on an arbitrage basis by improving the economics of US Gulf Coast export barrels out of Houston and Corpus Christi.
### Policy and regulation — PRICE-SUPPORTIVE (cyclical, escalation-dependent)
US policy posture has hardened. The administration has dismissed the prospect of near-term negotiations with Tehran and warned of broader military action including possible strikes on a suspected nuclear facility, alongside a pledge of retaliation for Houthi disruption of Red Sea shipping. The State Department has framed any future agreement as requiring guaranteed freedom of navigation through the Strait plus non-proliferation and non-support-for-militants commitments — a demanding set of conditions that lowers the near-term probability of a negotiated de-escalation. Strategic Petroleum Reserve policy remains a live but weakening lever: at 316.5 million barrels and a generally accepted operational minimum of 250–300 million, the room for a further meaningful release as a price-suppression tool is now limited.
### Macro, logistics and currency — PRICE-NEGATIVE (cyclical)
The dollar has advanced for four consecutive sessions and holds above 101, supported by ten-year Treasury yields above 4.6%. Policymakers have entered the pre-meeting blackout ahead of next week's Federal Reserve decision, at which rates are widely expected to be held; market pricing assigns roughly a 68% probability to an increase at the September meeting. A firm dollar and a hawkish rate path are both headwinds to a dollar-denominated commodity, and this factor is the principal macro offset to the supply story. Freight and insurance costs on Gulf and Red Sea routings are rising with the rerouting pressure, which raises delivered cost without necessarily raising benchmark price.
