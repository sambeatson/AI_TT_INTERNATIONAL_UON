# 5. Consensus Build Explanation
All six observations were normalised to a common basis before aggregation: front-month NYMEX settlement or last electronic trade, quoted in USD per barrel, Cushing delivery. Two adjustments were required. First, the TradingEconomics observation is explicitly an OTC/CFD reference rather than an exchange settlement — the publisher states this directly — so while its level is retained in the sample, it carries a reduced weight and its basis mismatch is declared rather than silently absorbed. Second, the intraday prints from Forbes, The National, and Investing.com timestamp progressively earlier points in a trending session; these were retained as path evidence but down-weighted relative to end-of-session observations, since a mid-session quote in a directional market is a statement about the path, not about the close.
The weighted median rather than the mean was used deliberately. In a session where price travelled $6.72 low-to-high, the arithmetic mean is dragged toward whichever part of the path happens to be over-sampled by the source set — here the earlier, lower prints — and would have understated the closing level by roughly $1.50. The median is robust to that sampling asymmetry.
One exclusion applies. OilPriceAPI is excluded entirely from the volatility pipeline for this instrument under a standing restriction arising from a basis-mismatch incident in an April session; its published series appeared in search results during this run and was not used at any stage. The final $92.08 figure is the most defensible number available because it is the level at which two independent end-of-session observations converge, on the correct contract basis, after the day's news had been fully absorbed.

This is the structural signature of a gap-and-go repricing on new information rather than a trend extension: the market opened, tested marginally lower, found no supply, and repriced in a single directional impulse.
## Sequence assessment
Five consecutive higher closes, five consecutive higher highs, and five consecutive higher lows. Each session's low sits above the prior session's low without exception — an unbroken ascending-low sequence over the full execution block. That is a technically clean advance with no failed rally attempts and no distribution.
The material qualification is the acceleration profile. Session-on-session gains run +$1.15, +$0.72, +$1.68, +$1.92, +$5.25. The final session is 2.7 times the largest preceding gain. Accelerating advances of this shape are characteristic of the terminal phase of an event-driven repricing rather than of a sustainable trend — the marginal buyer at $92 is paying a premium for a supply outcome that has not yet occurred, and if it does not occur, that premium has no floor beneath it other than the pre-event range. This does not invalidate the bullish reading, but it means the risk profile is now asymmetric in a way it was not on 20 July.

# 12. Key Market Considerations
## Supply — price-supportive (cyclical, escalating)
- Houthi militants struck two Saudi tankers, Encelia and Layla, in the Red Sea with missiles and drones, causing a fire aboard one vessel approximately 70 nautical miles southwest of Al Shuqaiq. Saudi state media confirmed the Encelia strike. These are the first direct tanker strikes in the Red Sea in this conflict.
- The Houthis declared a maritime blockade of Saudi ports. Five tankers changed course to avoid the Bab el-Mandeb Strait; Saudi oil loadings have reportedly dropped 36%. Asian refiners are rerouting Yanbu-loaded crude through Suez and around Africa, adding voyage time and freight cost.
- Kazakhstan suspended crude exports through the Caspian Pipeline Consortium terminal following drone attacks — a material non-OPEC supply interruption independent of the Gulf theatre.
- US forces conducted a twelfth consecutive night of strikes on Iranian targets. Hormuz tanker traffic is reported as near-halted.
- Saudi crude production stands at 6,637 kb/d in June, down from 7,010 kb/d — a decline of 373 kb/d that predates the current escalation and reduces available spare capacity to absorb further disruption.
## Demand — price-negative (cyclical, emerging)
- US crude inventories rose 2.0 million barrels to 411.7 million in the week ended 17 July, against a Reuters poll expecting a 1.1 million draw — a 3.1 million barrel miss against consensus. EIA attributed the build to easing refinery runs, lower crude exports, and higher imports.
- US weekly crude production eased to 13,798 kb/d from 13,861 kb/d — marginal, and not a supply constraint at this level.
- The equity complex is pricing demand destruction: the DAX fell 1.53% and the S&P 500 1.44% on the session, with European industrials underperforming.
## Substitution and spreads — price-supportive (cyclical)
- The Brent three-month timespread widened to $9.26, the widest since 22 May, deepening backwardation. This is the strongest single piece of evidence that prompt physical tightness is real rather than speculative.
- Brent trades at approximately $100.36 against WTI at $92.08 — a spread of roughly $8.28, wide by recent standards and consistent with the disruption being concentrated in seaborne rather than US domestic barrels. WTI is the relatively insulated benchmark here.
- Heating oil is up 4.33% on the session and 36.28% on the month; gasoline up 1.31% and 20.05%. Product strength confirms the move is transmitting through the refined barrel rather than being confined to crude paper.
## Policy and geopolitics — price-supportive (cyclical, high variance)
- The US President stated publicly that any Iranian attack on a vessel in the Strait of Hormuz would be answered by destroying one Iranian bridge or power plant, explicitly including targets in Tehran, and separately indicated he was considering a large-scale attack.
- Iran has threatened retaliation against US-linked energy infrastructure across the region.
- The US Secretary of State characterised Iran as not serious about peace talks; both capitals have ruled out near-term negotiations. The June ceasefire has collapsed.
## Macro and logistics — mixed
- The dollar index is stronger at 101.49, +0.36% — a headwind to dollar-denominated commodity demand that the move is currently overriding.
- US 10-year yields at 4.70% and 30-year at 5.11% represent tight financial conditions; the 10s-2s spread has steepened sharply.
- Freight and war-risk insurance premia on Red Sea transits are rising, adding a structural cost layer to delivered crude that persists independently of flat price.
