# §12 Key Market Considerations
*Updated specification*
## Supply — price-supportive
The Strait of Hormuz, through which roughly a fifth of seaborne crude flows, remains effectively disrupted; traffic has been at or near a standstill on tanker-attack risk and a US blockade on Iranian flows. The US is mid-way through releasing 172 million barrels from the Strategic Petroleum Reserve as part of a coordinated 400-million-barrel IEA action; the EIA reported the largest weekly SPR draw on record. SPR stocks have fallen toward the high-390-million-barrel area. This is the structural counterweight that keeps a floor under the market even as the risk premium deflates.
## Demand — price-negative
The IEA Oil Market Report flags a sharp demand contraction: global oil demand is now expected to fall by around 2.4 mb/d year-on-year in 2Q26, with Chinese seaborne crude imports down roughly 3.6 mb/d February-to-April and large cuts in Japan, Korea and India. Refiners have reduced runs and end-users are curbing consumption. The EIA STEO cut its 2026 demand-growth assumption to ~0.2 mb/d.
## Substitution & spread dynamics — neutral-to-negative
The Brent–WTI spread has been wide (Brent settled USD 103.54 versus WTI USD 96.60 on 22 May, roughly USD 7), reflecting the global-tightness-versus-US-supply split: Hormuz risk is more directly a Brent story. Crack spreads have been distorted by product-market tightness as the crude-market squeeze migrates downstream.
## Policy & regulation — mixed
Coordinated strategic-reserve releases are the dominant policy lever and are price-negative. Against that, the US blockade on Iranian exports and sanctions architecture remain supply-restrictive. The UAE announced its departure from OPEC effective 1 May 2026, a structural change to the cartel’s coordination calculus.
## Macro & market structure — price-negative
A firm US dollar near six-week highs, FOMC minutes showing most policymakers still open to further hikes if inflation persists, and markets pricing a possible 25bp hike by year-end together tighten conditions for the crude complex.
## Near-term catalysts
Iran’s review of the latest US proposal (delivered via Pakistani mediators); the next EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report (delayed to Thursday 28 May on the holiday schedule); any Hormuz shipping development; and OPEC+ supply signalling. Structural factors (Hormuz disruption, demand contraction) dominate the cyclical (headline-driven diplomacy swings) over the scenario horizon.
