# 12. Key Market Considerations
**Supply —** **Price-negative (structural)**:
Strait of Hormuz throughput is normalising — flows rose from a May low near 9.6 mb/d toward ~12 mb/d, Saudi tankers are restarting Persian Gulf exports, and a temporary US waiver permits purchases of already-loaded Iranian crude. US output is near a record ~13.6 mb/d (EIA). The IEA's June report flags a sizeable 2027 supply overhang (supply ~110 mb/d vs demand ~105 mb/d). This is the dominant driver of the current decline.
**Demand —** **Neutral-to-negative (cyclical)**:
Demand growth is modest (IEA/JPM ~0.9–2.0 mb/d for 2026) and is being compressed by the economic drag of a year of high energy prices. No acute demand catalyst is supporting price into the scenario horizon.
**Substitution / Spreads —** **Price-negative**:
WTI–Brent discount is ~$3.3 (WTI ~$70.4 vs Brent ~$73.7). Brent's prompt spread flipped into bearish contango on 24 Jun for the first time since the conflict began — a structural signal of near-term oversupply. Cushing stocks at ~19 mb are below operational comfort, a modest offsetting support.
**Policy / Geopolitics —** **Price-negative, with tail risk**:
A US–Iran framework signed in June and a 60-day ceasefire window have removed the war premium. Tail risk remains: any breakdown in the ceasefire, or the reported projectile incident near Oman, can re-introduce premium quickly (a structural-to-cyclical swing factor).
**Macro / FX —** **Price-negative**:
The dollar index printed a 13-month high mid-week (~101.7) on a hawkish Fed re-pricing (markets assigning ~63–68% odds of a September hike). A strong dollar is a direct headwind for dollar-priced crude, even as the index eased slightly into Thursday.
**Near-term catalysts —** **Watch**:
EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report; US PCE / Q1 GDP revisions; Michigan inflation expectations and Baker Hughes rig count (26 Jun); ceasefire-durability headlines. All are flagged in §13d.
