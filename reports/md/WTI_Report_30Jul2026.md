**§12 Key Market Considerations**
**Supply — price-supportive (cyclical, acute)**
The dominant force is a Middle East supply-disruption premium: renewed Houthi and Iran-linked attacks on Saudi oil infrastructure and Red Sea shipping, plus an unresolved Strait of Hormuz transit dispute. The EIA weekly report showed US commercial crude stocks fell 7.2 million barrels (week ending 24 July), reinforcing near-term tightness. **Demand — neutral (structural)**
No fresh demand catalyst. Equities were flat and industrial-demand proxies (DAX) gave no confirmation; the move is supply-led, not demand-led. Summer driving-season support is already in the price.
**Substitution / Spreads — supportive (cyclical)**
Brent settled around $90.5, keeping the Brent–WTI spread near $6, at the wide end — signalling that the global (waterborne) barrel is bid harder than landlocked WTI, consistent with a seaborne-supply-risk story. Heating oil and gasoline cracks are elevated, supporting refinery demand for crude.
**Policy / Geopolitics — supportive but two-sided (cyclical)**
Oman-brokered Hormuz diplomacy and intermittent US–Iran talks are the main bearish offset and the reason every rally has been sold; a credible transit agreement would remove several dollars of premium quickly. Strategic Petroleum Reserve policy is a latent supply lever.
**Macro / FX — mildly supportive (cyclical)**
A soft Dollar Index (~101.4) is a modest tailwind for USD-priced crude. The curve has shifted toward backwardation as the front premium builds.
**Near-term catalysts (see §13d)**
EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report; any confirmed Hormuz transit change; OPEC+ commentary; and further Red Sea / Saudi-facility incident headlines. All are flagged in the upcoming calendar.
