**§12 Key Market Considerations**
**Supply — PRICE-SUPPORTIVE (very strong).**
Strait of Hormuz effectively closed since late February under the US-Iran conflict; the IEA reports crude and fuel flows fell by roughly 4 million bpd in March and April, representing approximately one-fifth of global seaborne crude trade. Crude oil inventories — and Cushing storage specifically — posted larger-than-expected declines on the most recent EIA weekly. Spare capacity is materially constrained while the conflict continues. The IEA has warned the global oil market could remain materially undersupplied through October even if hostilities cease in the near term. (Source: EIA weekly petroleum status; IEA monthly Oil Market Report.)
**Demand — NEUTRAL TO MARGINALLY NEGATIVE.**
Chinese demand signals remain soft (April PMI sub-50) and rising prices are beginning to weigh on discretionary consumption. US summer driving season is approaching and refinery runs are at elevated rates, partially offsetting macro-demand concern. (Source: OPEC MOMR.)
**Substitution and spread dynamics — NEUTRAL.**
Brent-WTI spread sits around +3.84 USD/bbl with Brent at 109.26 — narrow versus historical averages, reflecting that the supply shock is global rather than purely seaborne; the WTI/Brent convergence implies US crude is being aggressively bid for export to Asia and Europe to fill the Gulf gap. Crack spreads (RBOB and Heating Oil) are widening, consistent with product-market tightness flowing back through to crude.
**Policy and regulation — PRICE-SUPPORTIVE.**
Sanctions waivers on Iranian oil have been allowed to lapse (per 16-May reporting). The US Strategic Petroleum Reserve refill posture is on hold; the Administration is actively exploring expanded federal-land leasing to compensate for lost barrels. OPEC+ supply discipline persists with the UAE having departed earlier in the year — implying less unified spare capacity to deploy. (Source: OPEC MOMR, US DoE.)
**Macro and market structure — PRICE-SUPPORTIVE.**
US CPI 3.8% YoY in April (sharpest reading since May 2023); PPI 6.0% YoY (largest monthly jump since March 2022). Headline energy contributed materially: CPI energy +3.8% MoM, PPI energy +7.8% MoM. Fed-funds-futures pricing now embeds one hike by March 2027 with >50% odds of a hike before year-end 2026 — a sharp pivot from the easing-bias path priced earlier in the year. The Cushing curve is in deep backwardation, consistent with physical tightness rather than financial-flow buying. (Source: BLS, Federal Reserve communications.)
**Near-term catalysts — PRICE-SUPPORTIVE BIAS.**
EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report due Wednesday 20-May; further inventory draws would extend the rally. Any change in Strait of Hormuz transit status — either reopening (sharply bearish) or further escalation (sharply bullish) — is the dominant binary risk. Diplomatic remarks from the Trump administration are an active live wire.
