[0:00-0:15]
Can a general-purpose AI produce market research that a regulated firm could trust, audit and repeat?

That was our question, with TT International, funded by UKFin+.
Show: Title card, logos
---
[0:15-0:30]
Our answer is a method we call CANON.

It started as TT's own governed template: change the brief, fix everything else.

We grew it into five parts: the brief, evidence rules, calculations, report layout and trade ideas.
Show: Three boxes growing to five
---
[0:30-0:55]
CANON stands for five commitments.

Constant: the method is locked during a run.

Architecture: it is modular, so each part can be checked on its own.

Normalised: every report uses the same basis, so markets compare like for like.

Output: every number traces back to a source or a calculation.

Nomenclature: fixed labels, so a reviewer always knows what a term means.
Show: CANON letter by letter
---
[0:30-0:55, continued]
Only the brief changes from report to report. Anything else changes only through a logged, versioned update.
Show: five boxes, "only this one changes" on the brief
---
[0:55-1:10]
Over twelve weeks it produced 324 daily reports across six markets: US, UK and Chinese equities, gold, oil and Bitcoin.

Each brings news sentiment, the economic calendar, price levels and trade ideas into one briefing.
Show: Report pages scrolling; six market labels
---
[1:10-1:25]
Real use tested the rules.

Data sources were blocked, so we required two sources for every price and nothing invented, and logged every failure as a numbered fix.
Show: Patch log; "flag, don't halt"
---
[1:25-1:55]
Then we tested the output.

We graded 171 reports for layout, calculations and sources.

For the S&P 500 and FTSE 100, we rebuilt the trade ideas using only data available before each trading day, and tested them three times over.

Layout was consistently strong; calculations and source checks were weaker.
Show: Poster chart: Layout / Calculations / Citations (switch to the findings background)
---
[1:55-2:15]
Strategy results were mixed.

Rebuilding the trade ideas gave no clear improvement, and higher quality grades were not associated with stronger trades.

Report quality and trading performance must be assessed separately.
Show: Poster panel 05; caption "no clear improvement"
---
[2:15-2:40]
So CANON is for use with professional review: analysts still verify sources and calculations.

What we leave behind is an instruction library, a testing toolkit, a report archive and a working paper.

Next: running CANON on a firm's own data, and packaging it as reusable skills.
